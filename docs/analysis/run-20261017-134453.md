# Assessment of run-20261017-134453

Analyzed 2026-10-18 from the downloaded `results.tar.gz`. The archive checksum
matches, and all 1,310 entries in the root and eight arm integrity inventories
match their files; the nine manifest hashes also match. This assessment uses the
archived execution evidence, not a live inspection of HiPerGator.

## Outcome

All eight configurations started and finished both selected articles: 16/16
attempts have predictions. The previous startup and RedHat quantizer failures did
not recur. All eight cleanup receipts record deletion of their target/draft
weights, serving images, and relevant runtime caches. Results and reasoning were
retained.

This is an operational success, but not a fully valid extraction campaign.
691/764 extraction tasks passed (90.4%); 43 failed and 30 were partial. Only three
article/configuration runs passed every extraction task. Those three were the
veterinary article under Muse Spark and the two Beam configurations. Six
article/configuration runs additionally reported measurement-series EHR errors.
The all-extraction-tasks-valid flag does not include those separate EHR stages.

The sample remains two **training** articles: PMC10998798.1, a feline case, and
PMC12134816.1, a four-patient human case series. Validation and test data were not
evaluated. One seed, unequal modality scope and recipe-specific GPU/concurrency
settings prevent definitive model, quantization, or speculation rankings.

## What remains wrong

### Timeline size and repair context

42/1,094 returned article calls (3.84%) ended with `finish_reason=length`, down
from 99/435 (22.8%) in the previous run. The set of successfully running models
changed, so this is a campaign-level comparison, not a controlled estimate of the
token-budget intervention. The same three configurations also improved: NVIDIA
MTP 35 to 3 truncations, LiLiCorr 39 to 5, and Beam NVFP4 25 to 7.

28 of the current 42 truncated calls were timelines. The other calls were
observations (5), demographics (4), measurement-series extraction (2), medications
(1), figure attribution (1), and care plans (1). Three timeline repair requests
were blocked before generation because the 65,536-token context had only 5,321,
9,846, or 14,960 output tokens available, below the configured 16,384 minimum.
Increasing the output request again will not resolve a full context window.

Temporal relations are especially weak on the four-patient human article:
timeline-relation F1 ranges from roughly 0.06 to 0.11 for every configuration,
despite much higher clinical-item F1. This deserves a separate quality target.

### Citations, schema, and repairs

Failures include nonliteral quotations, wrong segment IDs, missing visual
evidence fields, overlapping whole-figure/panel scopes, and changed or deleted
protected content during whole-object repairs. Panel inventory failures cascade
into blocked pixel attribution. Pixels were actually submitted in all vision
arms: 31 NVIDIA MTP, 33 BF16, 30 LiLiCorr, 26 Spark, and 33 RedHat image-bearing
article calls. Beam and Fastino sent none, as configured.

Spark's lone partial timeline illustrates a repair inefficiency. Its first
candidate labeled monitoring as a plan that had occurred. After one unparseable
repair, a subsequent repair changed another accepted event's description by
removing its duration; the protection gate rejected that unrelated change.
This supports editing only the invalid event or field, rather than regenerating
the whole timeline. It does not justify removing protection globally.

Measurement-series errors include offsets without both a unit and basis,
ownership citations specifying both/neither segment and cell, and truncated
outputs. The prompt's baseline-zero exception should be reconciled explicitly
with the validator's unit-and-basis requirement. When a time unit is unstated,
leave the numeric offset null rather than inventing one. All current
`series_points_f1` values are null; this run does not establish measurement-series
accuracy.

### RedHat speculative decoding

Summing the logged interval counters gives 213,889 accepted draft tokens out of
8,749,480 drafted, approximately **2.44%**. Other speculative configurations range
from roughly 27% to 66% in the same log analysis. The counters include server
probes and may omit the final unlogged interval; they are diagnostic evidence,
not an exact article-only metric.

Such low acceptance is a plausible contributor to RedHat's 1.66 docs/hour.
It does not by itself prove a wrong recipe or checkpoint. Before dropping FP8,
compare this exact target with speculation disabled and enabled, holding prompts,
sampling, GPU count, concurrency, and output budgets fixed. Audit the paired
drafter/tokenizer/configuration and capture article-boundary metrics. No
speculation-off baseline was run here, so speedup cannot yet be computed.

## Reasoning and scoring

All 1,094 returned article calls have saved text reasoning (820 initial and 274
repair calls). Across arms, 3,473 fact-index entries link facts/citations to
returned task-level reasoning. These entries are not necessarily distinct
clinical facts and are not per-fact proof that the reasoning is correct. Raw
traces remain in each article's extraction/EHR attempt ledgers. Synthetic startup
smoke reasoning is excluded from this count and from training records.

The existing scores use deterministic evidence-overlap and fuzzy-name matching,
with attributes scored separately. **No semantic LLM judge has been applied.**
Wording differences may produce false mismatches; overlapping evidence can also
produce overly generous matches. Therefore these are annotation-agreement
results, not adjudicated clinical accuracy.

## Recommended next work

1. **Use the saved outputs first.** Implement and calibrate a blinded semantic
   matching layer before another large extraction run. Candidate pairs should
   be judged against source evidence and patient identity, distinguishing
   equivalent claims, contradictions, related-but-different claims, and
   unsupported claims. Use one-to-one matching so duplicates cannot inflate
   recall. Preserve strict citation validation separately; a semantic judge
   must not legalize a fabricated quotation. Check unmatched predictions against
   the source to flag possible reference omissions rather than silently changing
   gold labels. Hand-review a stratified sample including disagreements and
   randomly selected agreements, measure judge errors, and freeze its model,
   prompt, and decisions before publishing semantic precision/recall/F1.
2. **Repair the observed workflow bottlenecks.** Split timeline event extraction
   and relation linking into bounded steps, with deterministic merging and
   explicit coverage checks. Repair only invalid atoms by stable ID while
   retaining accepted atoms. Make figure and measurement-series required fields
   and cross-field constraints explicit. Add regression fixtures from these
   failures; retain the original strict provenance/clinical gates. Track
   semantic correctness separately from schema validity.
3. **Run a small controlled RedHat throughput experiment.** Compare speculation
   on/off after auditing the paired recipe, and collect acceptance counters at
   article boundaries. Add startup-inclusive, answer-token, and valid-document
   throughput alongside total output tokens. Measure multiple client
   concurrencies only as a separately labeled serving experiment.
4. **Then expand to a representative development sample.** After a two-article
   regression smoke, use approximately 20–30 preselected training articles,
   stratified by patient count, source length, tables and images. Retain all eight
   requested configurations for the comparison, each once with images where
   supported. Beam NVFP4, Spark, and NVIDIA NVFP4 are sensible priorities for
   debugging, not declared winners. Keep validation/test reserved for the frozen
   protocol and report uncertainty at the article level.
5. **Distill only reviewed training examples.** Spark and Beam provide useful
   teacher candidates. Exclude or explicitly review failed, truncated,
   contradictory, or unsupported traces; keep accepted answers, citations,
   model/prompt identities and reasoning lineage. Do not train on validation/test
   cases. Compare answer-only versus answer-plus-returned-reasoning distillation
   on held-out accuracy and throughput rather than assuming longer reasoning
   improves a smaller model.

No serving behavior was changed as part of this assessment. These recommendations
require implementation and new validation before a full campaign; the archived
results remain unchanged.
