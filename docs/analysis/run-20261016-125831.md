# Super campaign assessment: run-20261016-125831

Analyzed 2026-10-17 from the locally supplied results.tar.gz. Archive SHA-256
matches its sidecar, and all 600 root/per-arm integrity entries match. The source
archive is unchanged. This was a two-article training-split smoke, not a validation
or test-set evaluation. It includes one veterinary case article and one article
with four human cases.

## Outcome and measured results

planned article runs. None of those six had every extraction task valid.

Rates use total completed-article tokens divided by total article time, including
repairs/EHR tasks and excluding startup/CPU work/queue. These are pipeline rates,
not single-request decoding speeds. Output tokens include reasoning. Valid-document
goodput is zero under the all-extraction-tasks-valid criterion. Including worker
startup/probes/cleanup, documents per GPU-hour fall to 0.90, 5.62, and 2.47 respectively.

Beam has the strongest provisional F1, particularly on the four-case article
(74.55% versus 57.60% for NVIDIA MTP and 54.25% for LiLiCorr). On the veterinary
article all three are low: 30.88%, 27.20%, and 26.98%. NVIDIA MTP has the best
observed GPU efficiency. LiLiCorr used concurrency 1 versus NVIDIA MTP's 64;
this is not an isolated comparison of speculative decoding algorithms. Beam also
has a different modality scope and four times as many GPUs. Two articles, one
seed, and unresolved task failures are insufficient to select a production winner.

Scoring is deterministic evidence-overlap/fuzzy-name matching with separately
scored attributes. No semantic LLM judge was run, and the reference is not claimed
to be physician-adjudicated gold.

99 of 435 calls (22.8%) hit exactly 16,384 output tokens. Reasoning used roughly
85–89% of all output tokens, and some truncated calls produced no final answer.
Observation extraction failed for all five patients under NVIDIA MTP, four under
LiLiCorr, and three under Beam. All five Beam timelines failed; truncation is a
major confounder in the recall comparison. Increasing budgets is justified, but
does not guarantee semantic correctness or eliminate every validation failure.

Additional errors include missing visual evidence fields, nonliteral citations,
repair attempts that erased protected content, and blocked attribution after
panel extraction failed. Both vision configurations had one measurement-series
EHR error. These gates should not be relaxed just to improve scores.

All 435 article calls saved returned text reasoning. There are 594 fact entries
with reasoning links (176 NVIDIA MTP, 180 LiLiCorr, 238 Beam), linked to task-level
calls and citations. These are candidate training records, not approved examples:
semantic correctness is unreviewed and training eligibility is unset. Exclude or
review truncated/invalid traces before distillation; preserve train/validation/test
separation. No Muse Spark clinical reasoning was generated because its server
never became ready.

The vision runs did send pixels: NVIDIA MTP had 33 image-bearing calls and
LiLiCorr had 31. Six figures were staged. Beam sent no images, as intended.

|---|---|---|
| Fastino | Harness killed loading at the 30-minute readiness deadline; 57/63 shards loaded | 90-minute readiness allowance |

| RedHat FP8 + DSpark | FP8 quantizer selected an embedding that the original config intended to exclude | Add the equivalent renamed vLLM embedding exclusion, preserving all original quantization settings and weight bytes |

Spark retains [Meta's recommended frontend and DSpark setup](https://dev.meta.ai/docs/muse-spark-open/deploy#vllm).

New runs request 32,768 tokens initially and on ordinary repairs; truncation
repairs may request 49,152, bounded by the exact remaining 65,536-token context.
Source text is never shortened. Requests need at least 16,384 available output
tokens. Request timeout is 20 minutes and article timeout 60 minutes. Reports now
expose pipeline validity and truncation counts separately from attempt completion.

All eight cleanup receipts record deletion of target/draft weights, serving SIFs,
and relevant caches. This is archived evidence of successful cleanup, not a live
inspection of the current cluster filesystem. Results and reasoning were retained.
