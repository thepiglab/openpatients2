These are item-detection agreement scores against the frozen AI-produced
reference, using the existing evidence-overlap/fuzzy matcher. They are not
clinician-adjudicated accuracy. A matched item can still have an incorrect
value, assertion, patient association or time. No semantic judge was applied.

| NVIDIA Super VL BF16 + MTP5, vision | 2 | 89.89% | 69.94% | 78.67% | 84.50% | 5.10 | 1,507 | 187 |

Throughput divides completed-article totals by summed article wall time, including
repairs and EHR calls and excluding downloads, queueing and server startup.
Output tokens include reasoning; answer tokens exclude it. Parallel task calls
make this an aggregate endpoint rate, not a single-stream decode speed. Token
counts also depend on each model's tokenizer. NVFP4 scheduled two independent
one-GPU replicas, but only one performed article work in this smoke test.

Beam has the highest recall and document throughput here; Spark has the highest
attribute agreement and fewer failed tasks. NVFP4 is the most economical measured
configuration per article GPU-hour (5.59 documents/GPU-hour, versus 2.69 Beam,
2.55 BF16 and 1.30 Spark, excluding startup). BF16 remains a useful precision
baseline, but this tiny sample does not show an advantage over NVFP4.
Keep all four for the development comparison. Do not use the small F1 differences
to declare a winner: only two articles, one seed, different modalities, recipes,
sampling and resource allocations were tested.

## Which articles were difficult, and why

| Article | Source characteristics | NVFP4 F1 | BF16 F1 | Spark F1 | Beam F1 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| PMC10998798.1 | One veterinary cat, parasitology; medium text; three figures and a table | .8324 | .7380 | .8020 | .8218 |
| PMC12134816.1 | Four pediatric hematology/oncology cases with venous thromboembolism; medium text; three figures and shared tables | .8027 | .8072 | .8288 | .8310 |

Every model found the expected original subjects: one cat and four children.
The observed difficulties concern tables, evidence and chronology; these two
papers cannot establish a general specialty ranking.

* **Missing table values:** Spark patient 2 and Beam patient 4 produced an FDP
  observation with a dash as `text_value`, `status=resulted` and
  `result_absent_reason=not_reported`. Those fields contradict each other.
  Repairs then tried to clear a unit or inferred specimen, hitting the value-loss
  guard. The prompt now explicitly distinguishes placeholders, actual results,
  reference ranges and documented specimen context. The guard remains strict;
  a model's wish to erase a field is not independent evidence that it is wrong.
* **Nonliteral evidence:** NVFP4's cat observations retained unresolved antigen
  test citations that did not match the cited segment. NVFP4's human figure
  tasks also had nonliteral quotes and panel-scope conflicts. Citation validation
  correctly refused them. These are candidates for selecting source segment IDs
  and exact spans before generating facts, rather than repeatedly rewriting
  quotations.
* **Contradictory timeline edges:** Beam had four partial patient timelines and
  Spark one. The old fallback discarded every edge when the combined graph
  contained a contradiction. The fix quarantines strict cyclic edges and
  inconsistent temporal constraint components, preserving independent valid
  relations. A replay of the five archived conflicting graphs retained **842
  of 1,039** previously discarded relations. This is structural/source recovery,
  not semantic validation; conflicts remain partial and original scores above
  were not recomputed with rewritten timelines.
* **Long and coupled output tasks:** BF16's patient-2 timeline-event batches
  included truncated/unparseable output; NVFP4's measurement-series EHR task
  exhausted its output budget. Figure and summary retries sometimes attempted
  to remove previously accepted atoms. Next target: smaller panel, claim and
  table-series units with stable IDs and bounded repair. A blanket token-limit
  increase would raise cost without fixing attribution.
* **Borrowed time:** Beam's patient-3 oncology task associated a remission time
  with evidence for a different fact. Keep the no-borrowing gate; repair or
  quarantine the association rather than accept an unsupported time.

Timeline event agreement was roughly .18–.45 and relation agreement mostly
0–.14. This deserves human review: the model/reference event granularity also
differs, so a low relation match is not automatically a wrong clinical sequence.

## Scheduling, resource evidence and cleanup

The download/run pipeline worked: NVFP4 ran while BF16 downloaded, and BF16 ran
while Beam downloaded. Downloads took approximately 30.5, 39.6, 49.1 and 59.4
minutes for NVFP4, BF16, Beam and Spark. The two-article workers were too short
to demonstrate sustained three-model overlap. NVFP4 replica 1 arrived after its
articles were already claimed/completed and exited without starting a server.

| Model | Server startup | Worker elapsed | Observed GPU-memory maximum | Observed cgroup RAM peak / request |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| NVFP4 | 17.1 min | 39.4 min | 174.2 GiB | 32.0 / 32 GiB |
| BF16 | 34.6 min | 59.0 min | 170.9 GiB | 64.0 / 64 GiB |
| Beam | 23.6 min | 35.5 min | 158.7 GiB, incomplete device coverage | 96.0 / 96 GiB |
| Spark | 14.3 min | 26.9 min | 151.4 GiB | 160.0 / 160 GiB |

Spark's worker started about 6h50m after its download finished. Scheduling is a
plausible explanation, but the archive lacks usable Slurm accounting to confirm
the cause. From the first download to the final cleanup was about 11 hours;
generation speed alone does not describe campaign turnaround.

Cgroup RAM includes file cache: the near-limit peaks are not process RSS and do
not demonstrate an OOM. The previous task cgroup reported an unlimited local
limit even when its ancestor was limited. New telemetry records the effective
ancestor limit, memory statistics and OOM-event counters. Do not reduce the
32/64/96/160 GiB requests from these old samples alone.

Beam's physical GPU IDs were remapped by the runtime; an incidental partial
match caused telemetry to record two of four allocated GPUs. The fix retains
all visible devices when their count matches the allocation and reports device
coverage explicitly. These changes make the next resource comparison usable.

All four cleanup receipts record deletion of model/drafter weights, SIF images
and model/download caches. Small client environments and uv caches are retained
by policy. The receipts support successful cleanup at that time; the archive
cannot certify current remote filesystem usage.

## Reasoning and code changes

All **940 article model calls** saved returned reasoning text. There are **3,047
fact-to-task-reasoning links** across the four outputs. Raw reasoning remains
in the extraction/EHR ledgers. These are task-level links, not independently
verified rationales for each fact; exclude failed, contradictory and unsupported
examples before distillation. No semantic judge or training filter ran here.

Implemented corrections:

1. Bootstrap the parent aggregate environment with locked core dependencies.
2. Collect packed child results before direct diagnostic export, even if scoring
   failed before collection; refuse export while lifecycle jobs are active.
3. Emit `summary/extraction-failures.json` per arm, including failed item repair
   history, task identity and source file paths.
4. Preserve independent timeline relations when a graph has conflicts.
5. Correct GPU visibility accounting and record useful cgroup memory breakdowns.
6. Clarify missing-value table extraction without weakening evidence/value gates.
7. Add experimental, source-only article-profile questions and exact evidence
   validation; see [the decision-model plan](../ARTICLE_PROFILING_DECISIONS.md).

Validation: 182 relevant tests passed, including the actual aggregate shell
launcher with a missing parent environment, packed export, timeline conflicts,
GPU remapping, memory accounting, failure indexing and article-profile contracts.
GPU serving and the new extraction prompt have not been rerun on HiPerGator.
