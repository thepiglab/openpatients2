Successful Living Kidney Donation from Heterozygous Carrier Parents to Siblings with Coenzyme Q8B Nephropathy: Two Case Series

[b00001] Abstract / Introduction
Coenzyme Q8B (COQ8B) nephropathy is an autosomal recessive hereditary disorder caused by primary coenzyme Q10 (CoQ10) deficiency. It manifests as a genetic steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome (SRNS), typically of childhood-onset. CoQ10 supplementation is a treatment option; however, it is not always effective in an entire patient population, leading to end-stage kidney disease. Kidney transplantation (KTx) is an effective treatment option for genetic SRNS; however, living KTx within biologically related members is associated with increased risk of allograft failure in recipients and future kidney dysfunction in donors. Here, we present two successful cases of living kidney donations from heterozygous carrier parents to their siblings with COQ8B nephropathy.

[b00002] Abstract / Case Presentation
The family comprised two parents and three siblings. Two of the daughters were diagnosed with proteinuria at 11 and 8 years of age, respectively. COQ8B nephropathy diagnosis was confirmed by next-generation and Sanger sequencing analysis, which revealed a novel compound heterozygous mutation in the COQ8B gene (c.737G>A and c.1468C>T). An older sister missed an opportunity for CoQ10 supplementation due to late diagnosis, whereas a younger sister did not respond to CoQ10 supplementation. Living kidney donation from father to the older sister and from mother to the younger sister was successfully performed without post-transplant recurrence in recipients or kidney dysfunction in donors within 5 and 2 years of follow-up.

[b00003] Abstract / Conclusion
Parent-to-child KTx may be an effective treatment option within family members affected with COQ8B nephropathy.

[b00004] Introduction
Coenzyme Q8B (COQ8B) nephropathy is a hereditary disease with mitochondrial dysfunction following primary coenzyme Q10 (CoQ10) deficiency, manifesting as steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome (SRNS) with histopathological findings of focal segmental glomerulosclerosis, typically during childhood or adolescence [–]. CoQ10 plays an essential role in maintaining proper function of the mitochondrial respiratory chain []. To date, several genes have been identified in humans known to cause primary CoQ10 deficiency along with COQ8B, such as COQ2, COQ4, COQ5, COQ6, COQ7, COQ8A, COQ9, PDSS1 (decaprenyl diphosphate synthase subunit 1), and PDSS2, whose products participate in CoQ10 biosynthesis [, ]. COQ8B is expressed in glomerular podocytes and partially localized in podocyte mitochondria and foot processes []. Podocytes are especially vulnerable owing to their high energy requirements, and dysfunction of CoQ10 biosynthesis in podocyte mitochondria results in the breakdown of the glomerular filtration barrier []. A global study has been conducted to prove the renoprotective effect of CoQ10 supplementation in patients with SRNS having hereditary CoQ10 biosynthesis deficiency due to biallelic pathogenic variants in either COQ2, COQ6, or COQ8B genes [], though not always effective in the entire population, and some patients progress to end-stage kidney disease (ESKD).

[b00005] Introduction
Kidney transplantation (KTx) is a curable option for patients with COQ8B nephropathy having ESKD, and its extrarenal manifestation in COQ8B nephropathy is rare compared with other genetic mitochondrial cytopathies [–]. KTx is categorized as either a living or deceased kidney donation. Reflecting on lack of deceased donors, as much as 87.6% of kidney donations rely on living donors in Japan []. However, in chronic kidney disease cases with a genetic background, transplant practitioners should be aware that these candidates may be potential heterozygous carriers or, in worst case scenarios, carriers of late-onset kidney diseases []. Living KTx with biologically related members has been reported to increase the risk of allograft failure in recipients and future kidney dysfunction in donors [, ]. Here, we report 2 successful cases of living kidney donation from heterozygous carrier parents to siblings with COQ8B nephropathy after 5 and 2 years of follow-up.

[b00006] Case Presentation / Case 1: Kidney Donation from Father to an Older Sister (Recipient 1)
The family comprised 2 parents and 3 siblings. The parents and older brother had no history of kidney disease or extrarenal manifestations (Fig. 1a). An 11-year-old girl was diagnosed with proteinuria during a school urinary screening program, but she did not undergo further examination. At 18 years of age, she was referred to our nephrology department with an elevated serum creatinine (1.5 mg/dL). The patient did not present with any extrarenal manifestations. A kidney biopsy was not performed because of kidney atrophy. The patient was lost to follow-up, and when she was admitted to our hospital for loss of appetite at 19 years of age, she had progressed to ESKD, demonstrating the need for hemodialysis. She eventually underwent living KTx from her father when she was 20 years old. A zero-time renal biopsy revealed normal findings including podocyte mitochondria. Considering the rapid progression of kidney failure observed in this patient and proteinuria identified in her younger sister, next-generation and Sanger sequencing analysis was conducted to determine the origin of the kidney disease, which revealed a novel compound heterozygous mutation in the COQ8B gene (c.737G>A in exon 9 and c.1468C>T in exon 15) (Fig. 1b), leading to a diagnosis of COQ8B nephropathy (Fig. 2a). Five years after the KTx, recipient 1 developed de novo IgA nephropathy, which was successfully treated with prednisolone pulse therapy. The kidney function was serum creatinine 0.90 mg/dL and eGFR 63 mL/min/1.73 m^(2) at the final follow-up with normal urinalysis. The electronic microscopy of the transplanted kidney revealed normal findings of mitochondria in the podocytes, which denied post-transplant recurrence in the kidney graft. Kidney function of the donor was serum creatinine 1.24 mg/dL and eGFR 48 mL/min/1.73 m^(2) with normal urinalysis at the final follow-up.

[b00007] Case Presentation / Case 2: Kidney Donation from Mother to Younger Sister (Recipient 2)
The clinical course of recipient 2 is shown in Figure 2b. An 8-year-old girl was diagnosed with proteinuria. The patient did not present with any extrarenal manifestations. Recipient 1’s progression to ESKD prompted the family and the younger sister to decide to undergo further evaluation for her proteinuria, and a kidney biopsy was performed at 11 years of age. Five of 30 glomeruli were sclerotic, and five showed segmental sclerosis, which was consistent with focal segmental glomerulosclerosis (Fig. 3a, b). Granular swollen epithelial cells were also observed in tubular cells (Fig. 3c). Electronic microscopy revealed foot process effacement and a marked increase in mitochondria of abnormal shape and size in the podocytes, with the presence of microvillus transformation (Fig. 3d, e). A diagnosis of COQ8B nephropathy was confirmed based on genetic mutation patterns identical to those of her older sister. CoQ10 supplementation therapy was initiated at a dose of 5 mg/kg/day, followed by a gradual increase to 30 mg/kg/day, although her kidney function continued to deteriorate with an increase in urinary protein and beta-2-microglobulin. The addition of lisinopril at a dose of 2.5 mg per day was not beneficial, and the patient progressed to ESKD. Considering the advantages of preemptive KTx compared with hemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis followed by deceased KTx, she proceeded with living KTx from her mother at 16 years of age, 4 years after initiating treatment. Two years after the KTx, kidney function of recipient 2 was serum creatinine 1.02 mg/dL and eGFR 60 mL/min/1.73 m^(2). Post-transplant disease recurrence was not observed in the protocol kidney biopsy. Kidney function of the donor was serum creatinine 0.93 mg/dL and eGFR 49 mL/min/1.73 m^(2) with normal urinalysis.

[b00008] Discussion
We documented 2 siblings with COQ8B nephropathy, in which a novel mutation pattern of compound heterozygous mutation in the COQ8B gene (c.737G>A and c.1468C>T) was confirmed by next-generation and Sanger sequencing analysis. A late diagnosis of COQ8B nephropathy deprived the older sister of the opportunity for CoQ10 treatment, while the younger sister demonstrated little response to CoQ10 supplementation despite early diagnosis. Although kidney donation from biologically related donors is generally associated with a higher risk of allograft failure in recipients and future kidney dysfunction in donors, living KTx from the parents as heterozygotes of COQ8B mutation to the COQ8B nephropathy siblings was uneventfully performed without post-transplant recurrence in the recipients or kidney dysfunction in the donors.

[b00009] Discussion
A global cohort study demonstrated that supplementation with CoQ10 for primary CoQ10 deficiency due to biallelic pathogenic variants in either the COQ2, COQ6, or COQ8B genes led to better preservation of kidney function []. However, a nephroprotective effect was not observed in a subgroup of patients with COQ8B deficiency in this cohort, which had possibly been related to the typically late diagnosis and more advanced chronic kidney disease at treatment initiation. Song et al. [] retrospectively assessed 20 patients with COQ8B nephropathy and reported a significantly better kidney survival in early genetic detection group than that in delayed genetic detection group, enhancing the importance of early treatment. In fact, documented kidney responses vary from no response, partial remission, or complete disappearance of urinary protein [–], influenced by multiple factors such as delay in diagnosis, timing of therapy initiation, comorbid conditions, and variants in mutation []. Moreover, nonadherence rate is reported to be as high as 30–70% in pediatric populations [], making the treatment more complicated. In the present cases, late diagnosis of COQ8B nephropathy deprived the older sister of the opportunity for CoQ10 treatment, whereas the younger sister demonstrated little response to CoQ10 supplementation despite early diagnosis. Regarding the reasons for treatment failure, the younger sister was diagnosed at an early stage and received an adequate dose of CoQ10 supplementation. As for genetic mutations, this combination of compound heterozygous mutations (c.737G>A and c.1468C>T) is, to the best of our knowledge, the first genotype pattern reported to date, which may have been one of the factors for unresponsiveness. On the other hand, CoQ10 supplementation would have been effective if it had been initiated at an even earlier stage, that is, before the increase in proteinuria or serum creatinine. Nonadherence remains a differential diagnosis. An accumulation of cases is required to better understand the response to CoQ10 treatment in patients with COQ8B.

[b00010] Discussion
The outcomes of KTx for genetic SRNS are generally excellent. Morello et al. [] reported that the risk of recurrence rate in patients with idiopathic nongenetic SRNS who underwent KTx was as high as 53.3–61% [], while that in patients with genetic SRNS was significantly low. Zeng et al. [] focused on the outcomes of KTx for COQ8B nephropathy and revealed that none of the 18 recipients experienced post-transplant recurrence. A limitation of these studies is the lack of information about kidney donors, raising the question of whether patients with COQ8B nephropathy are not at risk for post-transplant recurrence after KTx from heterozygous carriers. To date, only 2 case reports have described parent-to-child KTx in patients with COQ8B nephropathy, with limited follow-up period of 0–2 years (Table 1) [, ]. Our two COQ8B nephropathy recipient cases demonstrating the long-term effectiveness of kidney donation from related heterozygous carrier donors may be encouraging for both clinicians and affected families in decision-making.

[b00016] Discussion
In general, the risk of living kidney donors developing ESKD is 3.5–5.3-fold higher than the projected risk in the absence of donation []. Transplant practitioners should pay utmost attention to the screening and selection of potential living donor candidates, especially in the clinical setting for kidney disease with a background of genetic disorders []. This is because living donors who are biologically related to the recipient have a higher risk of future kidney failure after kidney donation than unrelated donors []. However, transplant centers follow a case-by-case approach rather than a standard protocol for genetic assessment because of the lack of reliable evidence []. For example, it has been universally recognized that mutations in COL4A3 or COL4A4 genes are responsible for autosomal recessive Alport syndrome. However, individuals with pathogenic heterozygous COL4A3 or COL4A4 variants have recently been diagnosed with thin basement membrane nephropathy or autosomal dominant Alport syndrome, and heterozygotes carrying certain mutation patterns are at a risk of future kidney dysfunction. In fact, the current guidelines for genetic testing and management of Alport syndrome state that COL4A3 and COL4A4 heterozygotes are not recommended as kidney donors in the development of late-onset kidney failure [–]. COQ8B nephropathy, similar to Alport syndrome, is an autosomal recessive hereditary kidney disease, though SRNS develops only in homozygotes and compound heterozygotes. To our knowledge, no cases of COQ8B nephropathy have been reported in individuals heterozygous for COQ8B variants. Our zero-time kidney biopsy specimens showing unaffected mitochondria in the podocytes and 5 or 2 years of an uneventful clinical course without dysuria or kidney dysfunction post-donation support the hypothesis that heterozygotes for COQ8B mutation can safely donate their kidneys. Nevertheless, the prognosis of COQ8B heterozygous donors has not been clarified so far, making it more important for our donors to maintain regular follow-ups.

[b00017] Discussion
In conclusion, we reported two recipients and donors who underwent successful parent-to-child kidney donation among family members with a novel mutation pattern in COQ8B gene, yet relatively short follow-up periods and extremely small sample size are limitations in this study. Living KTx is an effective treatment option for patients with COQ8B nephropathy.
