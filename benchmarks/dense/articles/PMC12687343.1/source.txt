Successful outpatient parenteral antimicrobial therapy with rezafungin for chronic pulmonary aspergillosis: a case report and health economic assessment

[b00001] Abstract / Background
Chronic pulmonary aspergillosis (CPA) is a serious lung disorder caused by Aspergillus fungal infection with high morbidity and mortality. Treatment options include antifungal azoles, polyenes and echinocandins. Rezafungin is a novel, once-weekly echinocandin that may benefit CPA management in outpatient parenteral antimicrobial therapy (OPAT) services.

[b00002] Abstract / Objectives
To report a case where rezafungin was potentially effective in managing CPA for a patient with limited treatment options, and to estimate the health economic impact of rezafungin versus once-daily echinocandin in an OPAT setting.

[b00003] Abstract / Patients and methods
We discuss a patient with CPA, diagnosed in 2020, with frequent episodes of haemoptysis, dyspnoea, productive cough and lethargy with progressive bilateral pulmonary cavitatory lesions and nodules treated with rezafungin over 12 weeks via OPAT. Costs to deliver once-weekly rezafungin compared with once-daily caspofungin in the OPAT clinic were compared.

[b00004] Abstract / Results
Symptoms improved significantly after 12 weeks of rezafungin, with no further emergency visits, versus eight in the year prior due to CPA symptoms. Aspergillus serology (IgG) improved and computerized tomography imaging showed slight improvement in the degree of consolidation surrounding the cavities. Daily OPAT costs increased by £13.71 for rezafungin compared with caspofungin. This was offset by greater patient convenience and improved OPAT clinic capacity.

[b00005] Abstract / Conclusions
Rezafungin may be a novel, well-tolerated and cost-effective echinocandin treatment for CPA in patients needing OPAT and with limited treatment options. Further clinical studies are required for robust evaluation of this novel echinocandin in CPA.

[b00006] Background
Chronic pulmonary aspergillosis (CPA) is a long-term Aspergillus infection of the lungs, usually caused by the fungus Aspergillus fumigatus (AF). Patients are typically immunocompetent with pre-existing lung damage by diseases such as bronchiectasis, tuberculosis, asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease. The most common form is chronic cavitatory pulmonary aspergillosis (CCPA); characterized by one or more cavities with or without fungal balls growing inside, causing symptoms for three or more months. Symptoms include chronic productive cough, dyspnoea, haemoptysis and weight loss; some patients are asymptomatic.

[b00007] Background
Isolation of Aspergillus species from sputum is not diagnostic of CPA, as it may reflect airway colonization. CPA diagnosis requires: (i) imaging showing one or more lung cavity (with/without fungal balls) and/or nodules; (ii) mycological evidence of Aspergillus infection or an immunological response to Aspergillus species and (iii) exclusion of alternative diagnoses, all present for ≥3 months.

[b00008] Background
The British Thoracic Society clinical statement guides CPA management in the UK. Long-term antifungal therapies are recommended and there are three classes: azoles, polyenes and echinocandins. First-line treatment is with oral azoles (itraconazole or voriconazole); second-line are posaconazole or isavuconazole for intolerance, toxicity or resistance to first-line therapy. Azole resistance is increasing due to widespread agricultural azole fungicide use, selecting strains with cyp51A gene mutations, notably the TR34/L98H polymorphism. A UK study found resistance in 86% of AF isolates to itraconazole, 64% to voriconazole, 18% to posaconazole, 83% to isavuconazole, 51% to three azoles and 14% to all azoles. Resistance, side effects and drug–drug interactions limit azole use.

[b00009] Background
Intravenous (IV) polyenes (e.g. liposomal amphotericin B, LAB) and echinocandins (caspofungin and micafungin) are alternatives.^(,) LAB causes renal impairment in up to 25% of patients with CPA. Echinocandins are better tolerated and work by inhibition of 1,3-β-D-glucan synthase complex, an essential component for fungal cell wall synthesis. Caspofungin and micafungin are given once-daily IV; guidelines recommend a 6-month course for CCPA if tolerated.

[b00010] Background
Outpatient parenteral antimicrobial therapy (OPAT) services enable IV antimicrobial delivery in patients’ homes or in dedicated hospital clinics. Once-daily IV echinocandins are used in OPAT clinics for treatment of deep seated fungal infections including CPA, but are time-consuming, labour intensive and restrictive for ambulant patients. Rezafungin, a newly-licensed echinocandin, has a prolonged half-life (>130 hours), permitting once-weekly dosing. A case series suggested good tolerance to rezafungin and effective mycological clearance in Candida infections. Other authors report rezafungin use is associated with a shorter length of hospital stay and fewer intensive care admissions versus caspofungin. Furthermore, once-weekly rezafungin is non-inferior to caspofungin for all-cause mortality in candidaemia and invasive candidiasis. Although no clinical data exist, rezafungin demonstrated comparable in vitro activity against Aspergillus. A phase 2 trial of weekly rezafungin for 6 months in CPA is recruiting (NCT06794554).

[b00011] Background
Here we present a successful case of managing progressive CPA with once-weekly rezafungin via OPAT.

[b00012] Patients and methods / Case report
A 57-year-old Caucasian female from Scotland, diagnosed with CPA in May 2020, presented to A&E eight times within 1 year with CPA symptoms (acute, small volume haemoptysis, cough productive of green sputum, dyspnoea and lethargy). Past medical history includes allergic bronchopulmonary aspergillosis, Pseudomonas aeruginosa colonization, asthma, bronchiectasis, osteoporosis with vertebral compression fractures and gastro-oesophageal reflux disease. Medications included solpadol, paracetamol, lidocaine plaster, ondansetron, salbutamol inhaler and nebulizer, carbocisteine, prednisolone 5 mg once-daily, salmeterol/fluticasone inhaler, peptac, calcium and vitamin D3, lansoprazole, Qvar 50 inhaler, hydroxocobalamin injections, and zolendronate infusions. She is a never smoker with no occupational exposures.

[b00013] Patients and methods / Investigations
Minor bronchiectatic changes appeared in the upper lobes on computerized tomography (CT) in 2012, with the first lung cavity in the left upper lobe seen in 2014. By 2015, CT revealed bilateral cavities and nodules, with progressive worsening over time. The most recent CT performed in October 2024 before rezafungin demonstrated persistent bilateral upper lobe cavitating lesions, new nodularity with tree-in-bud opacification, and multiple aspergillomas, some newly formed and others enlarged compared with previous imaging (Figure 1).

[b00014] Patients and methods / Investigations
Serum IgG to Aspergillus was raised (Figure 2) and she had positive sputum Aspergillus PCR on three separate occasions. Sputum culture in January 2020 isolated AF, sensitive to LAB, itraconazole, voriconazole and caspofungin. Aspergillus antigen was positive in bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) samples in 2018, 2019 and 2021 with optical density indices (ODI) of 1.63, 0.91 and 0.96, respectively (normal range <0.5 ODI). BAL mycobacterial cultures and cytology were negative. Vasculitis and autoantibody screens were negative. Pseudomonas was isolated from sputum in 2024. She had never received a surgical lung biopsy as she was deemed too high risk.

[b00015] Patients and methods / Treatments pre-dating rezafungin
Voriconazole, started in 2017, was stopped after 1 week due to visual hallucinations. Oral itraconazole 200 mg twice daily was given throughout 2019 but discontinued in December 2019 due to disease progression on CT. Oral posaconazole was started in 2019 (and monitored levels were therapeutic) but was stopped in September 2020 after detection of the TR34/L98H resistance mutation on pyrosequencing from sputum samples. Isavuconazole was avoided due to this resistance. A 3-week course of IV LAB (3 mg/kg once-daily) in 2020 led to clinical improvement. From November 2021 to January 2024, LAB infusions were given as required, based on symptom deterioration and rising IgG to Aspergillus.

[b00016] Patients and methods / Treatments pre-dating rezafungin
Owing to limited treatment options, the case was discussed at the UK’s National Aspergillosis Centre multidisciplinary team meeting. A three-month course of IV echinocandin was advised given clinical and radiological deterioration. The patient declined inpatient IV caspofungin due to risks associated with prolonged admission. Home treatment was not possible due to absence of a community OPAT service in her area. She was therefore referred to NHS Greater Glasgow and Clyde’s OPAT service to explore outpatient options. Daily IV caspofungin via OPAT would have required significant travel (48 miles/day) and administration time. In view of these logistic barriers, weekly rezafungin via the OPAT clinic was agreed.

[b00017] Patients and methods / Treatments pre-dating rezafungin
A loading dose of 400 mg IV rezafungin was followed by 11 200 mg IV weekly doses given between January and April 2025. Weight remained stable at 44 kg throughout. There were no drug–drug interactions noted. Symptoms reported during treatment included nausea, anorexia and myalgia, especially in the 24 hours immediately post-infusion. There were several episodes of lightly blood-tinged sputum over the treatment but no frank haemoptysis and no antibiotics were prescribed. Full blood count, renal and liver function tests were monitored throughout the course remained stable. A creatine kinase level was normal at week nine. A UK Medicines and Health Care Products Regulatory Agency (MHRA) yellow card report was submitted in view of treatment-associated myalgia. A 3-month treatment course was decided on at MDT (rather than 6 months) based on lack of published data for this new agent.

[b00018] Patients and methods / Treatments pre-dating rezafungin
Total treatment costs were calculated and compared between rezafungin (weekly) and caspofungin (daily) based on administration in the OPAT clinic (see Figure 2c and figure legend for detailed costings).

[b00019] Results
At the time of publication, the patient reports increased energy, reduced dyspnoea, decreased sputum production, significantly less haemoptysis and improved mood. Weight has increased to 47.6 kg. Treatment-related myalgia resolved over several weeks. There have been no A&E attendances since rezafungin completion, compared with eight in 1 year preceding treatment, all with CPA symptoms.

[b00020] Results
Interval CT chest with contrast 2 weeks after rezafungin completion showed small improvement in the bilateral cavities. No new cavities or nodules were seen (Figure 1). Aspergillus IgG decreased from 165 mgA/L in January 2025 to 98 mgA/L in September 2025 after rezafungin (normal 0–40 mgA/L) (Figure 2).

[b00021] Results
Twelve-week costs were estimated as £8454.32 for weekly rezafungin versus £7302.11 for daily caspofungin, translating to an additional £13.72/day (or £32.23/day if patient-incurred travel costs were excluded). Rezafungin costs were predominantly from drug acquisition whereas nursing and travel time contributed significantly to caspofungin costs (Figure 2).

[b00022] Discussion
To our knowledge, this is the first report of rezafungin monotherapy in CPA treatment. There has been one case report of its use in combination with voriconazole to treat azole-refractory pulmonary aspergillosis.

[b00023] Discussion
Treatment of CPA is challenging because of limited therapeutic options, significant drug interactions and emergence of resistance.^(,,) Treatment goals are to prevent disease progression and improve symptoms and quality of life. While azoles are first-line, the patient struggled with side effects from azoles and her AF isolate developed azole resistance. Similar to rezafungin, LAB also lends itself well to weekly or intermittent dosing because it has a long half-life (127–152 hours) and favourable pharmacokinetics. However, there were concerns about nephrotoxicity and in our setting, administration was limited to inpatient care. Echinocandin therapy was therefore considered most appropriate. Weekly rezafungin was advantageous, protecting the patient from risks associated with hospital admission and avoiding daily OPAT clinic visits. OPAT is proven cost-efficient for six common clinical conditions and delivery models versus inpatient management. While rezafungin reduces length of hospital stay and therefore costs in deep seated candidiasis, no published economic evaluation exists for its use in OPAT. In this case, weekly administration raised daily costs by £13.72 but decreased travel by 288 miles per week and healthcare time by 9 hours per week. This optimized nursing time and increased clinic capacity to support early discharge and admission avoidance for other patients; potentially enabling 72 extra infusions over the 12 weeks.

[b00024] Discussion
Although limited as a single case, this report suggests rezafungin improved symptoms, was reasonably well-tolerated and required no treatment interruptions. CT confirmed stability and slight improvement in radiological parameters with 3 months’ treatment. The optimal length of rezafungin treatment for CPA remains to be elucidated.

[b00025] Discussion
Combination therapy trials for CPA are needed, as monotherapy has a ∼60% response rate and frequent drug resistance. Once-weekly rezafungin will facilitate further evaluation with azoles. New agents such as olorofim and manogepix also show promise, especially against azole-resistant AF. Although echinocandins are fungistatic while azoles and LAB are fungicidal, micafungin and caspofungin have established utility in CPA, with response rates comparable to LAB.

[b00026] Discussion / Conclusions
This report illustrates once-weekly rezafungin as a potentially cost-effective treatment for CPA when oral treatment options are not available. A phase 2, multicentre, open label, single arm study to evaluate the effectiveness and safety of rezafungin in the treatment of CPA in patients with limited treatment options is recruiting (NCT06794554) and will better explore this treatment strategy.
