Clinical Outcomes of Traction-Assisted Endoscopic Submucosal Dissection for Prepyloric and Pyloric Neoplasms: Propensity Score Matching Analysis

[b00001] Abstract / Background/Aims
Traction-assisted endoscopic submucosal dissection (TA-ESD) using dental floss substantially reduces procedural times without increasing adverse event rates. However, few studies have described the clinical outcomes following TA-ESD for prepyloric and pyloric neoplasms. Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the clinical outcomes of patients treated with TA-ESD for prepyloric and pyloric neoplasms.

[b00002] Abstract / Methods
We retrospectively analyzed the data of 208 patients who underwent ESD for prepyloric and pyloric neoplasms between 2016 and 2021 at Pusan National University Hospital. The patients were categorized into the conventional ESD (C-ESD) (n=173) and TA-ESD (n=35) groups. One-to-two propensity score matching (PSM) was performed between both groups.

[b00003] Abstract / Results
In the unmatched cohort, compared with the C-ESD group, the TA-ESD group had a significantly greater rate of resection defects exceeding half of the circumference of the pylorus (45.7% vs 27.2%, p=0.048), a lower complete resection rate (85.7% vs 97.1%, p=0.014), a higher positive vertical margin rate (5.7% vs 0.0%, p=0.028), and a longer procedural time (34.2±26.1 minutes vs 25.3±22.0 minutes, p=0.036). After PSM, the TA-ESD group required a significantly shorter procedural time than did the C-ESD group (30.1±18.1 minutes vs 40.4±23.7 minutes, p=0.031). No statistically significant differences were observed in other variables between the two groups.

[b00004] Abstract / Conclusions
Compared to C-ESD, TA-ESD significantly shortened the procedural time for treating prepyloric and pyloric neoplasms. However, no significant differences were observed in the complete resection rate or in adverse events between the two groups.

[b00005] INTRODUCTION
Endoscopic resection for gastric neoplasms was first attempted in 1974 for treating pedunculated or semi-pedunculated gastric cancers. In 1984, Tada et al. performed polypectomy using a snare, naming the procedure a “strip-off biopsy.” Subsequently, in 1988, Hirao et al. developed a technique in which a mixture of epinephrine-hypertonic saline was injected into the lesion, followed by circumferential mucosal incision and resection with a snare. Endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD) was first performed in the late 1990s. The advent of ESD has expanded the indications for endoscopic treatment of early gastric cancer, and ESD has been established as a standard treatment for gastric epithelial neoplasms., ESD is recognized for its effectiveness in achieving complete resection of tumors. However, ESD for gastric neoplasms located in difficult positions, such as the pylorus or cardia, remains challenging for endoscopists. In the prepyloric and pyloric regions, the procedural field is narrow, peristalsis limits the working space, and scope movement is often restricted. Endoscopists have explored various strategies to mitigate the difficulty of high-risk ESD. Traction-assisted ESD (TA-ESD) is an approach designed to reduce the difficulty of lifting the resection flap using dental floss or thread attached to the lesion. The clip-with-line method, reported by Oyama in 2002, is presumed to be the first reported traction method., Although the efficacy of traction methods in gastric ESD has been demonstrated in various studies, studies analyzing the efficacy of TA-ESD for prepyloric and pyloric neoplasms remain limited. Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the clinical outcomes of TA-ESD for prepyloric and pyloric neoplasms.

[b00006] MATERIALS AND METHODS / 1. Patients
We reviewed the medical records of 225 patients (231 lesions) who underwent ESD for prepyloric and pyloric lesions between 2016 and 2021. Of the 231 lesions, 17 were excluded owing to the absence of adenoma or gastric cancer in the histopathologic specimen after ESD. In addition, when a patient underwent ESD for two lesions, only the larger lesion was included, resulting in the exclusion of six lesions. Consequently, 208 lesions from 208 patients were included in the final analysis (Fig. 1). The patients were classified into the TA-ESD and conventional ESD (C-ESD) groups. In the C-ESD group, no additional devices were used beyond the ESD knife (Flex, Dual, or IT knives; Olympus Co., Ltd., Tokyo, Japan) and hemostatic forceps (Coagrasper; Olympus Co., Ltd.) after the actual dissection began. This study was reviewed and approved by the Institutional Review Board of Pusan National University Hospital (IRB number: 2411-018-145). The requirement for written informed consent was waived by the IRB.

[b00007] MATERIALS AND METHODS / 2. Definition
The pyloric type (P-type) was defined as a lesion in which its distal margin was located within 0.5 cm of the pyloric ring, whereas the prepyloric type (PP-type) was defined as a lesion in which its distal margin was located between 0.5 cm and 1.0 cm from the pyloric ring. The P-type also included lesions that extended beyond the pyloric ring to the duodenal side.

[b00008] MATERIALS AND METHODS / 2. Definition
Procedural time was defined as the duration from the start of marking the resection borders to the complete resection of the lesion. To define the directional distribution, the greater curvature of the pyloric ring was designated as 6 o’clock and the lesser curvature as 12 o’clock on the endoscopic screen, creating four quadrants. These quadrants were defined as follows: first (12−3 o’clock), second (3−6 o’clock), third (6−9 o’clock), and fourth (9−12 o’clock) quadrants. If the lesion was large enough to involve multiple quadrants, the quadrant containing the midline of the lesion was designated as the directional distribution of the lesion. The circumferential extent of resection was measured by connecting the two lateral endpoints of the mucosal defect after resection to the center of the pyloric ring, and the angle formed by these two lines was used as the criterion. En bloc resection was defined as complete resection of the lesion in a single piece, while complete resection was defined as an en bloc resected specimen that was histologically confirmed to have tumor-free horizontal and vertical margins.

[b00009] MATERIALS AND METHODS / 2. Definition
Adverse events related to the procedure were defined as follows: bleeding was defined as the occurrence of melena or hematochezia after the procedure, endoscopically confirmed bleeding within 24 hours post-procedure, or bleeding severe enough to require blood transfusion. Perforation was defined as an endoscopically diagnosed perforation during the procedure or radiological evidence of perforation identified after the procedure. Stenosis was defined as the inability of a standard 10 mm diameter endoscope to pass through the pylorus during endoscopic evaluation after the procedure.

[b00010] MATERIALS AND METHODS / 3. Traction-assisted ESD
All ESD procedures were performed at the Pusan National University Hospital by four experienced endoscopists (G.H.K., B.E.L., M.W.L., and G.A.S.), each of whom had previously performed >100 ESD procedures. The endoscope used for the procedures was a single-channel scope (GIF-H260, GIF-Q260, or GIF-HQ290; Olympus Co., Ltd.). All procedures were performed under conscious sedation, beginning with the intravenous administration of 5–10 mg midazolam and 25 mg meperidine. Additional sedation was provided intravenously with propofol if needed during the procedure. Throughout sedation, the consciousness, blood pressure, pulse rate, and oxygen saturation of the patient were closely monitored.

[b00011] MATERIALS AND METHODS / 3. Traction-assisted ESD
The first step of the procedure involved assessing the lesion boundaries using white-light endoscopy or chromoendoscopy. The resection borders around the lesion were marked using argon plasma coagulation. After marking, a small amount of 0.9% normal saline solution, mixed with epinephrine and indigo carmine, was injected into the submucosal space beneath the lesion. After the submucosal injection, a circumferential mucosal incision was made using an ESD knife, and submucosal dissection was performed. In the C-ESD group, complete removal of the lesion was achieved solely through dissection with a knife. In the TA-ESD group, the traction method was applied during the dissection (Fig. 2). The traction method was applied as follows: (1) the endoscope was withdrawn from the patient; (2) an endoclip was inserted through the endoscope channel until the tip of the clip emerged from the opposite end; (3) dental floss was tied to one tip of the endoclip, and the clip was retracted back into the channel; (4) the endoscope was reinserted and guided back to the lesion, ensuring that the untied end of the dental floss remained outside the body of the patient; (5) the endoclip was attached to the flap of the lesion, where submucosal dissection was partially performed, ensuring it did not capture the muscle layer. The endoclip with dental floss was placed in the direction most favorable for further submucosal dissection; or (6) once the endoclip was positioned, the external end of the dental floss was manually adjusted to control traction on the flap, allowing for continued submucosal dissection.

[b00012] MATERIALS AND METHODS / 4. Statistical analysis
The primary outcome was the procedural time. Secondary outcomes included en bloc and complete resection rates, adverse event rates, and predictive factors influencing complete resection. A logistic regression model was used to calculate propensity scores based on age, sex, location (P-type or PP-type), directional distribution, circumferential extent of resection, tumor size, presence of ulcers, macroscopic findings, and histopathology. One-to-two propensity score matching (PSM) between the TA-ESD and C-ESD groups was performed using nearest-neighbor matching with a 0.2 caliper, yielding 33 and 66 matched cases, respectively. Matching quality was assessed using standardized mean differences and displayed using a Love plot (Supplementary Fig. 1).

[b00013] MATERIALS AND METHODS / 4. Statistical analysis
Variables are expressed as means with standard deviations or simple proportions. To assess statistical significance, the chi-square test or Fisher exact test was used for categorical variables and the Student t-test for continuous variables. Multivariate logistic regression was performed in the PSM cohort to identify factors affecting complete resection. A p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical analyses were conducted using R version 4.1.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) and SPSS software, version 25.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

[b00014] RESULTS / 1. Baseline characteristics of both groups
Of the 208 patients, 173 underwent C-ESD, and 35 underwent TA-ESD. Before PSM, no statistically significant differences were observed between the two groups regarding age, sex, location, directional distribution, tumor size, macroscopic findings, presence of ulcers, and histopathology. However, lesions with a resection extent exceeding half of the circumference were more common in the TA-ESD group than in the C-ESD group (45.7% [16/35] vs 27.2% [47/173], p=0.048). After PSM, no significant differences were observed in any variable, including the circumferential extent of resection, between the two groups. The absolute standardized mean differences for all nine variables were less than 0.2, with seven of them being less than 0.1, indicating that the matched results were overall well balanced. Patient characteristics are summarized in Table 1.

[b00015] RESULTS / 2. Procedural outcomes
Before PSM, the procedural time required for the TA-ESD group was longer than that required for the C-ESD group (34.2 minutes vs 25.3 minutes, p=0.036). However, after PSM, the procedural time required for the TA-ESD group was significantly shorter than that required for the C-ESD group (30.1 minutes vs 40.4 minutes, p=0.031). No differences were observed in the adverse event rates between the two groups before and after PSM in terms of bleeding, perforation, or stricture.

[b00016] RESULTS / 2. Procedural outcomes
Regarding histopathological outcomes, before and after PSM, no differences were observed between the TA-ESD and C-ESD groups. Similarly, no differences were observed in the en bloc resection rates between the two groups. Before PSM, the complete resection rate was lower in the TA-ESD group than in the C-ESD group (85.7% vs 97.1%, p=0.014), with a higher positive vertical margin in the TA-ESD group (5.7% vs 0.0%, p=0.028). However, no significant difference was observed in the complete resection rate between the two groups after PSM (87.9% vs 97.0%, p=0.093). The procedural outcomes are summarized in Table 2.

[b00017] RESULTS / 3. Predictive factor for complete resection
Univariate analysis of factors associated with complete resection indicated that lesions with a resection extent exceeding half of the circumference tended to be associated with a lower complete resection rate (odds ratio [OR], 0.121; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.014 to 1.076; p=0.058). Other factors, such as location, directional distribution, macroscopic findings, tumor size (≤20 mm or >20 mm), histopathology, presence of ulcers, and method, were not significantly associated with complete resection. Multivariate analysis revealed that the complete resection rate tended to be lower in lesions where the resection extent exceeded half of the circumference (OR, 0.149; 95% CI, 0.016 to 1.426; p=0.099). However, TA-ESD did not significantly impact the complete resection rate (OR, 0.221; 95% CI, 0.119 to 1.474; p=0.119) (Table 3).

[b00018] DISCUSSION
In this study, TA-ESD for prepyloric and pyloric neoplasms significantly shortened the procedural time compared with C-ESD after PSM. However, no differences were observed in the en bloc and complete resection rates, as well as adverse event rates between the TA-ESD and C-ESD groups. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to evaluate the clinical outcomes of using the traction method for prepyloric and pyloric neoplasms.

[b00019] DISCUSSION
Previous randomized controlled trials have demonstrated the benefits of the traction method in gastric ESD; TA-ESD significantly shortened the procedural time compared to C-ESD for lesions located in the middle or upper stomach.- These results suggest that TA-ESD is particularly effective in certain regions of the stomach. However, these studies categorized lesion locations into upper/middle/lower stomach and lesser/greater curvature/anterior/posterior sides and did not evaluate the effect of TA-ESD for specific sites such as pyloric or cardiac neoplasms. These studies demonstrated that TA-ESD did not reduce procedural time in the lower stomach; however, our study demonstrated that TA-ESD significantly shortened the procedural time for pyloric or prepyloric lesions. A recent meta-analysis comparing C-ESD and TA-ESD demonstrated no significant difference in procedural time between C-ESD and TA-ESD in the stomach. However, other clinicopathologic factors, such as tumor location, lesion morphology, tumor size, or presence of an ulcer, were not included in this meta-analysis.

[b00020] DISCUSSION
The prepyloric and pyloric regions pose technical challenges for ESD owing to the narrow procedural field, limited endoscope control, and peristaltic movements of the pyloric muscle, making ESD more complex., Therefore, TA-ESD may be more effective in the prepyloric and pyloric regions than in other regions of the stomach. Given the retrospective nature of our study, TA-ESD may have been performed more frequently on technically challenging lesions where achieving complete resection was challenging; specifically, C-ESD might have been switched to TA-ESD during the procedure for these lesions. The frequency of lesions for resections extending beyond half of the circumference was higher for the TA-ESD group than for the C-ESD group (45.7% vs 27.2%, p=0.048), which may explain the longer procedural time in the TA-ESD group than that in the C-ESD group (34.2 minutes vs 25.3 minutes, p=0.036), as well as the lower complete resection rate in the TA-ESD group than that in the C-ESD group (85.7% vs 97.1%, p=0.014) before PSM. After PSM, the procedural time in the TA-ESD group was significantly shorter than that in the C-ESD group (30.1 minutes vs 40.4 minutes, p=0.031), and no significant difference in the complete resection rate was observed (87.9% vs 97.0%, p=0.093). Our results expand the effectiveness of TA-ESD to the prepylorus and pyloric areas.

[b00021] DISCUSSION
In this study, no significant difference in adverse event rates was observed between the C-ESD and TA-ESD groups. These results are consistent with those of previous studies indicating that TA-ESD is safe for gastric neoplasms.-, One case of perforation occurred in the TA-ESD group; however, it was a small perforation, which was immediately managed using endoclips. Thus, TA-ESD did not significantly reduce the risk of perforation compared to C-ESD, as observed in the study by Yoshida et al. This could be because all operators in the study were highly experienced endoscopists who were skilled at avoiding muscle layer injury during submucosal dissection, even without using traction methods. The incidence of bleeding can also be explained on similar grounds. However, submucosal dissection during C-ESD is presumed to require more meticulous attention than during TA-ESD, potentially leading to a longer procedural time. Consistent with our findings, Nagata et al.'s study involved a single highly experienced endoscopist performing all procedures, with no reported cases of perforation. Involving less experienced endoscopists might alter the procedural outcomes. Therefore, further studies are warranted to assess the efficacy of traction methods for prepyloric and pyloric neoplasms performed by less experienced endoscopists. Post-procedural stenosis rates did not differ between the two groups. Circumferential involvement of over three-quarters of the pylorus is a significant risk factor for stenosis. Although local steroid injection has been suggested as a preventive measure, supporting evidence remains limited.

[b00022] DISCUSSION
Similar to previous studies,- this study found no significant differences in the complete resection rate between the two groups. However, a recent retrospective study reported that the use of traction methods during full-thickness resection of gastrointestinal stromal tumors significantly increased the R0 resection rate compared to C-ESD (84.4% vs 51.0%, p=0.002). This suggests that traction methods may be more effective for achieving complete vertical resection.

[b00023] DISCUSSION
Multivariate analysis indicated that a circumferential extent of resection exceeding 1/2 tended to be associated with low complete resection (OR, 0.149; 95% CI, 0.016 to 1.426; p=0.099). This finding implies that as the circumferential extent of resection increases, the likelihood of achieving complete resection decreases.

[b00024] DISCUSSION
We used a technique in which dental floss tied to a clip was pulled out through the esophagus and mouth to provide traction during all the procedures. The method, known as the dental floss clip (DFC) method, is a commonly used approach in TA-ESD. The direction of the traction force in this method varies depending on the location of the lesion. Two previous retrospective studies (one involving PSM analysis) confirmed that this method effectively reduces the procedural time for ESD., However, in another randomized controlled trial, this method did not significantly reduce procedural time. Unlike the DFC method, other traction methods allow for directional control of the traction force. The spring-and-loop with clip traction (SLC) method permits free adjustments of the traction direction.,, In a randomized controlled trial comparing C-ESD and ESD using SLC, the procedural time was shorter for the ESD using SLC group than for the C-ESD group. However, in this study, no correlation was observed between the reduction in procedural time and ESD for lesions >20 mm. This may be because, as the procedure progresses, the distance between the SLC attachment site and the flap decreases, reducing the traction force. Therefore, the DFC method, which allows for external adjustment of traction tension, might be advantageous for large lesions. Unfortunately, because we used the DFC method for all procedures, we could not investigate whether DFC or SLC was superior for ESD of prepyloric and pyloric neoplasms. Further studies on this topic should be conducted.

[b00025] DISCUSSION
This study had some limitations. Although we attempted to minimize bias through PSM, the retrospective nature of the study remained an inevitable limitation. Notably, the use of TA-ESD was mainly determined by the preference of the endoscopists; specifically, two endoscopists (G.H.K. and M.W.L.) performed more TA-ESD than the other endoscopists (B.E.L. and G.A.S.). Second, two cases in which the initial C-ESD was changed to TA-ESD during the procedure were included in the TA-ESD group. Due to the retrospective nature of this study, it was not possible to determine the exact timing of conversion from C-ESD to TA-ESD. However, as there were only a small number of cases (two cases) in which the initial C-ESD was changed to TA-ESD, their impact on the overall results is likely minimal. Third, the number of patients included in this study was substantial (n=208); however, the final number of included cases after PSM was relatively small (n=99). Therefore, further large-scale prospective studies comparing planned TA-ESD with C-ESD for prepyloric and pyloric neoplasms are required to validate our findings. Fourth, this study did not include any cases of SLC-assisted ESD, and we did not perform a subgroup analysis of procedural time by lesion size. Therefore, we could not determine which traction method is better for resecting large pyloric lesions. Further research is warranted to compare traction methods and to identify lesion characteristics associated with their effectiveness. Fifth, several potential confounding factors, including fibrosis, lesion depth, previous intervention, and operator fatigue, were not accounted for in the analysis. Finally, due to the retrospective nature of this study, the exact time required for clip band attachment could not be confirmed, and comparison based solely on dissection time was not possible. However, since the total procedure time including clip band attachment was shorter in the TA-ESD group, it can be inferred that the dissection time itself was also shorter. Considering these points, our conclusion that TA-ESD shortens the overall procedure time remains supported.

[b00026] DISCUSSION
In conclusion, TA-ESD significantly shortened the procedural time for prepyloric and pyloric neoplasms compared to C-ESD while maintaining comparable safety profiles and complete resection rates. Therefore, the prepyloric and pyloric regions represent additional locations where TA-ESD is effective, similar to its efficacy in the upper and middle stomach.
