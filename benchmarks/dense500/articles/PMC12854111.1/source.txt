Memantine Use in Prolonged Cognitive Sequalae in Pediatric Anti-N-methyl-D-aspartate Receptor Encephalitis: A Three-year Follow-up Case Report and Brief Review

[b00001] Abstract
Anti-N-methyl-D-aspartate receptor encephalitis (anti-NMDAR encephalitis) is an autoimmune encephalopathy increasingly recognized in the pediatric population. Although conventional immunotherapy-focused treatments have significantly improved outcomes, a substantial proportion of patients experience enduring neuropsychiatric and cognitive sequelae. Here, we present a three-year longitudinal follow-up report of a female case with confirmed anti-NMDAR encephalitis, focusing on the neuropsychiatric trajectory and cognitive sequelae management with memantine. A previously healthy 10-year-old girl presented with acute-onset psychotic, affective and cataplexy-like features, primarily such as severe irritability and dysphoria, mixed-type mood fluctuation, affective lability, disorganized speaking and thinking, mutism, visual hallucinations, cognitive regression, dysgraphia, and impulsive behavioral dysregulation. The patient received immunotherapy including intravenous methylprednisolone, intravenous immunoglobulin, and rituximab, as well as concurrent psychotropics, including valproate and olanzapine/aripiprazole. Despite psychiatric improvement in 6 months, she showed persistent deficits in memory, executive function, and impulse control, even after 18th month of conventional therapies. Despite appropriate immunotherapy and psychotropic treatments, exhibited persistent deficits in attention, memory, and executive functioning (dysfunction in language and learning), without any severe adverse events. Memantine, a non-competitive NMDAR antagonist with documented neuroprotective properties, was initiated during the chronic phase as complementary to psychotropic medications. While 20 mg/day dose of memantine was administering during 6 months, marked improvement was observed in her verbal fluency, academic functioning, and social engagement, that may be associated with post-acute initiation of memantine alongside conventional treatments. This case highlights the evolving understanding of post-autoimmune cognitive rehabilitation and discusses current evidence and theoretical rationale supporting memantine use in complementary treatment of prolonged cognitive dysfunction in the pediatric anti-NMDAR encephalitis.

[b00002] INTRODUCTION
Anti-N-methyl-D-aspartate receptor encephalitis (anti-NMDAR encephalitis) is an autoimmune disorder first described in 2007, typically characterized by a multi-phase neuropsychiatric presentation, including acute psychosis, memory deficits, cognitive impairment, speech difficulties, epilepsy, movement disorders, and autonomic instability []. Anti-NMDAR encephalitis is a rare but increasingly recognized autoimmune disorder, with an estimated incidence of 1.5 cases per million per year, disproportionately affecting children and young adults []. While initially characterized in young women with ovarian teratomas, it is now well-recognized in children of both sexes and most likely to affect pediatric population, particularly females [].

[b00003] INTRODUCTION
Clinically, it presents with a distinct constellation of psychiatric, cognitive, and motor symptoms, often in a stereotyped sequence. Psychiatric symptoms such as agitation, hallucinations, and bizarre behavior are typically the earliest signs and may mimic primary psychiatric conditions, especially in pediatric populations []. Pediatric presentations often feature a prodromal viral-like illness followed by acute behavioral regression, emotional dysregulation, aggression/agitation, mutism, sleep dysfunction and catatonia rather than frank psychosis, which is more typical in adults [].

[b00004] INTRODUCTION
The condition arises from autoantibodies targeting the GluN1 subunit of N-methyl-D-aspartate receptors (NMDAR), particularly in hippocampal neurons, impairing synaptic plasticity and long-term potentiation—mechanisms essential for learning and memory. Long-lasting and often severe psychiatric impairments represent the core symptoms of anti-NMDAR encephalitis [], which have been explained with the disinhibition of excitatory glutamatergic pathways within fronto-subcortical neuronal circuits [,]. The underlying pathophysiology involves anti-NMDAR antibodies, leading to receptor internalization and hypofunction, disrupting glutamatergic neurotransmission []. This NMDAR dysfunction disproportionately impacts GABAergic interneurons, causing disinhibition of excitatory circuits and contributing to neuropsychiatric manifestations []. In children, the developing brain’s vulnerability to disruptions in fronto-subcortical circuits—crucial for executive functioning, emotion regulation, and attention—may exacerbate the long-term cognitive and behavioral sequelae observed even after immunological remission []. Therefore, anti-NMDAR-E serves not only as a critical clinical entity but also as a model for understanding NMDA-mediated mechanisms underlying pediatric neuropsychiatric disorders.

[b00005] INTRODUCTION
Early immunomodulatory treatment has improved prognosis; however, long-term cognitive and psychiatric sequelae remain prevalent, negatively affecting school performance, social cohesion and quality of life []. According to 2021 international consensus guidelines, current treatment protocols in children include first-line immunotherapy with intravenous methylprednisolone (IVMP), intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG), and/or plasma exchange (up to 12−18 months), followed by second-line immune-modulating agents such as rituximab or cyclophosphamide in refractory or relapsing cases which are about 12−15% of the affected cases [-]. While most children regain functional independence, 75−90% report persistent deficits in executive function, cognition, and psychosocial adjustment [-]. Predictors of poor outcomes and prolonged cognitive sequalae include delayed immunotherapy, intensive-care unit admission, prolonged seizures, and early age at onset [,]. Despite immunological remission with conventional treatment approaches, the lingering neuropsychiatric deficits such as residual cognitive and executive dysfunction remain under-addressed in treatment protocols and necessitate adjunctive interventions [,-]. In recent years, despite there have been numerous adult case reports on the dichotomic outcomes of memantine in the management of anti-NMDAR encephalitis, there is still lack of consensus about the use of memantine in pediatric anti-NMDAR encephalitis, indicating requirement of more reports to clarify the efficacy and potential limitations of memantine use in affected children with various neuropsychiatric conditions [].

[b00006] INTRODUCTION
Memantine, an uncompetitive and reversible NMDAR antagonist with a low-to-moderate affinity approved for Alzheimer’s disease, has shown promise in modulating glutamatergic dysfunction and enhancing neuroplasticity with a neuroprotective effect on neuronal survival [-]. Its pharmacokinetics involve partial trapping of the NMDAR, being mediated by neuronal membrane depolarization and fluctuating occupancy depending on the glutamate concentration []. Memantine modulates the overall activity of prefrontal glutamate and dopamine neurotransmission, and exhibits neuroprotective effects by modulating excessive NMDAR activity, which prevents an influx of neurotoxic calcium ions and increases the release of neurotrophic factor from astroglia in the brain []. The hypothesis on the benefit of memantine in the cognitive and behavioral impairment in several neuropsychiatric condition may depend on regulation of prefronto-striatal glutamatergic-dopaminergic neurotransmission imbalance and exertion of neuroprotective outcomes by modulating glutamatergic neurotransmission [].

[b00007] INTRODUCTION
Though off-label, memantine has been trialed in various pediatric neurodevelopmental and psychiatric conditions. Memantine have generally suggested as well-tolerated in pediatric samples and may have potential clinical benefits in children with a broad range of pediatric neurodevelopmental disorders, particularly autism spectrum disorder (ASD) [] and attention deficit-hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) [,]. Therapeutic efficacy and long-term tolerability of both immediate-release form [-] and extended-release form of memantine [] has been reported in the core social communication/interaction symptoms of ASD. However, the efficacy of memantine in core ASD symptoms were contrast reported as suspicious, despite of its benefits on acute symptoms of anxiety and other comorbid symptoms []. On the other hand, memantine has recently emerged as a prominent treatment option in the epileptogenesis, epileptic encephalopathy and long-term sequelae of epilepsy due to its potential anti-inflammatory effect [,-]. Moreover, recent studies have highlights on its efficacy as an adjunctive option in improving cognitive/negative symptoms of schizophrenia, however with requirement of further investigations on the efficacy and safety in bipolar and major depressive disorders [].

[b00008] INTRODUCTION
This case presents an in-depth trajectory of a pediatric anti-NMDAR encephalitis patient with notable long-lasting cognitive impairment, with a focus on the post-acute phase well-tolerated memantine treatment. The case aims to contribute to the growing literature on the tolerability and potential cognitive benefits of memantine in children with autoimmune encephalitis.

[b00009] CASE
A previously healthy 10-year-old female presented in April 2022 with abrupt neuropsychiatric symptoms, including visual hallucinations (e.g., seeing spiders on surfaces), severe irritability, disorientation, dysgraphia, affective instability, and obsessive-compulsive behaviors. She exhibited catatonic/cataplexy-like episodes (mutism, akinesia, unresponsiveness, bizarre and inappropriate smiling), social withdrawal and academic decline. Following ineffective treatment with risperidone (1 mg/day for 6 months) at an external facility, she was referred to our child psychiatry clinic quite late in September 2022. Due to elevated prolactin levels and poor clinical response, risperidone was discontinued after first evaluation in our clinic.

[b00010] CASE
A multidisciplinary diagnostic evaluation was initiated in collaboration with pediatric neurology. Broad laboratory screening, including toxicology (plasma heavy metal levels, including zinc, lead, copper, and ceruloplasmin, as well as 24 hours total urine copper level) global immunological and autoimmune markers (anti-TPO, ANA, anti-dsDNA, Antistreptolysin-O, etc.), metabolic, infectious, endocrine, and oncological panels (including pelvic and abdominal ultrasonography, especially focusing on oncological etiologies), was unremarkable. Only Herpes-siplex Virus 1 IgG was elevated, and a recent viral respiratory infection and lip herpes (in February 2022) was reported by caregivers. Initial neurological exams and imaging at the referring center were non-focal. However, repeated cranial magnetic resonance imaging (c-MRI) in our clinic (April 2023) revealed bilateral hippocampal hyperintensities on T2-FLAIR (Fig. 1A). Cerebrospinal fluid analysis showed low titer of NMDAR antibody positivity (titer 1:100), confirming the diagnosis of anti-NMDAR encephalitis. Treatment included five-day courses of IVMP (1 g/day) and IVIG (2 g/kg), followed by three additional cycles and rituximab (375 mg/m^(2), ×4 weekly) due to partial immunotherapy response. Concurrently, valproate (15−20 mg/kg/day) and olanzapine (10 mg/day) were initiated to manage psychiatric symptoms. Over 12 months, sertraline (25 mg/day) was added, and olanzapine was replaced with aripiprazole (15 mg/day) to manage side effects, such as elevated weight and sedation.

[b00011] CASE
By the 18th month of follow-up with conventional treatments, substantial improvement was observed in aggression, affect regulation, and psychosis. Repeat c-MRI at 24th month of conventional treatments showed decreased hippocampal signal intensity bilaterally (Fig. 1B). However, the patient continued to present with persistent cognitive deficits in almost all dimensions of executive functions, particularly significant in memory, attention, and abstract reasoning, as well as verbal fluency and learning. Executive dysfunction was also evident in daily functioning, reflected in both clinical interviews, and neurocognitive testing scores, including The Behavior Rating Inventory of Executive Function-Parents version (BRIEF-P) and Wechsler Intelligence Scale for Children-Revised (WISC-R) (Tables 1, 2). In September 2024, with psychiatric stabilization and no further autoimmune relapses, memantine was introduced at 10 mg/day, titrated to 20 mg/day due to sustained dysfunctions in executive skills. Following the 6th months of memantine initiation, the patient exhibited improved verbal output, reduced obsessions, better sustained attention, and enhanced academic performance (grades increased to 80%). The 6-month change in test-retest outcomes, particularly improvements exceeding 50% in BRIEF-P subscales such as emotional control, working memory, organization of materials, and monitoring—reflecting meta-cognition, verbal fluency, and attention—demonstrates observable clinical improvement. The changes in WISC-R subtests related to verbal IQ (specifically memory) and age-adjusted subtests like picture completion, picture arrangement, block design, and coding were evaluated as objective indicators of improvement in domains such as attention and problem-solving abilities. Parent-proxy reports indicated improved autonomy and social functioning, with notable reductions in BRIEF-P scores. Notably, mild side effects including nausea and hyperactivity resolved spontaneously.

[b00012] CASE
Despite the presence of significant and observable subjective and objective improvements, it is not possible to claim that these changes are solely or directly attributable to memantine use based on this single-case study evaluating individual characteristics and treatment outcomes. The continued administration of first-line and second-line conventional therapies, the potential for spontaneous clinical resolution over time, and changes in environmental psychosocial support represent covariates whose effects on the observed improvement cannot be isolated or disregarded.

[b00013] CASE / Psychometric Assessment Tools
The WISC-R assessed cognitive domains and yielded three composite scores: full-scale IQ, verbal IQ, and performance IQ []. The BRIEF-P, a parent-reported measure, evaluated executive functions, particularly the reflections on daily functioning, across eight subscales (inhibition, shifting, emotional control, working memory, plan/organize, initiation, organization of materials, and monitoring) and global indices (global executive composite, behavioral regulation index, and total score). Cultural validation of both instruments was previously established for the Turkish population [,].

[b00014] CASE / Psychometric Assessment Tools
Informed consent was obtained from the patient’s legal guardians. Long-term psychiatric follow-up continues in our outpatient clinic.

[b00015] DISCUSSION
While early immunotherapy significantly improves survival and stabilization in pediatric anti-NMDAR encephalitis, a considerable proportion of affected children continue to experience enduring neurocognitive sequelae, particularly in memory, attention, and executive functions, during the chronic phase [,]. Recent multicenter neuropsychological follow-up studies have documented persistent deficits in academic performance, social interaction, verbal fluency, and behavioral self-regulation even after apparent psychiatric recovery, underscoring the need for targeted cognitive rehabilitation strategies beyond acute care [].

[b00016] DISCUSSION
In their recent systematic review, Liu et al. [] examined the literature on memantine use across various age groups diagnosed with anti-NMDAR encephalitis, analyzing treatment outcomes in eight cases. The review aimed to outline the memantine’s potential efficacy and tolerability. However, findings predominantly centered on observable gross outcomes such as catatonia that persisted despite conventional therapy, offering little insight into cognitive domains—particularly executive functions—which require comprehensive neuropsychological evaluation and follow-up []. Although this study represents one of the most current and prominent works on memantine use in anti-NMDAR encephalitis, it does not address its effect on cognitive sequelae.

[b00017] DISCUSSION
Reports addressing prolonged cognitive sequelae in anti-NMDAR encephalitis are largely limited to case reports and small case series. These studies emphasize the heterogeneous presentation of cognitive deficits across individuals and stress the importance of early intervention, but they seldom propose treatment strategies [,-,]. In a longitudinal case series, Finke et al. [] described persistent cognitive deficits—particularly in memory and executive functions—during the post-acute phase in nine patients. Notably, those who received early diagnosis and immunotherapy demonstrated significantly better cognitive recovery compared to delayed cases. Their findings support the need for additional interventions in cases like ours, where diagnosis and treatment initiation were delayed, and cognitive sequelae persisted despite appropriate immunotherapy. Hinkle et al. [] presented a comparative analysis of three adolescent girls with anti-NMDAR encephalitis, assessing behavioral and neuropsychological functioning across acute, post-acute, and chronic phases following first-line therapy. Their findings revealed that despite conventional treatment, persistent cognitive deficits—especially in executive functioning, memory, language, and learning—were evident, varying in pattern across individuals.

[b00018] DISCUSSION
While these key studies stress the importance of early diagnosis and highlight persistent cognitive impairments in certain cases, data on targeted therapeutic strategies for rapid and effective resolution of such sequelae are limited. Few studies have discussed adjunctive approaches. For example, Mohammad et al. [] focused on symptomatic management of agitation, sleep disturbances, seizures, and behavioral symptoms in anti-NMDAR encephalitis but did not include cognitive sequelae in their treatment discussion. Similarly, Bradley [] reported on neurorehabilitation strategies for persistent post-acute cognitive deficits in adults with anti-NMDAR encephalitis despite standard therapy, but did not elaborate on interventions for pediatric patients or those with delayed treatment.

[b00019] DISCUSSION
Based on the earlier evidence and reports on the tolerability and benefit of memantine in pediatric neuropsychiatric disorders [,,], memantine may serve as an effective adjunctive strategy in managing prolonged cognitive sequelae in anti-NMDAR encephalitis during the post-acute phase, particularly when recovery stagnates despite standard therapy. In summary, memantine was selected not as a first-line intervention but as a targeted, adjunctive approach in a post-acute and chronic-phase pediatric anti-NMDAR encephalitis patient with demonstrable cognitive sequelae. Selection of memantine in this condition was based on: (a) neurobiological plausibility (mechanistic alignment of memantine with anti-NMDAR encephalitis) [,]; (b) prior pediatric safety data (clinical precedents for memantine use in pediatric neurodevelopmental disorders) [,,]; (c) clinical need (lack of alternative pharmacological options in the post-acute and chronic phases) [,,]; (d) the lack of established alternatives (therapeutic gap identified in the literature) [,,].

[b00020] DISCUSSION
This case contributes to that unmet clinical need by illustrating the possible benefit of memantine in a pediatric patient with residual cognitive deficits following standard conventional immunotherapy and psychotropic treatment. Memantine’s mechanism—noncompetitive NMDAR antagonism—may counteract glutamatergic dysregulation caused by antibody-mediated receptor internalization, supporting synaptic remodeling and neurocognitive recovery [,]. Although off-label, memantine has shown potential in neurodevelopmental disorders such as ASD, ADHD, obsessive compulsive disorder, and schizophrenia, with minimal adverse effects and a favorable drug interaction profile [,,]. Its application in anti-NMDAR encephalitis has been reported in limited case series and retrospective reviews. Liu et al. [] described eight patients (mean age 26.1 years) treated with memantine during the chronic or catatonic phase, five of whom showed cognitive and functional improvements. However, two experienced symptom exacerbation, highlighting the need for individualized risk-benefit assessment due to memantine’s potential dual effects on receptor activity.

[b00021] DISCUSSION
Memantine is typically started at 5−10 mg/day and titrated to a maximum of 20 mg/day in pediatric use. Side effects are usually mild, including gastrointestinal symptoms, agitation, or sleep disturbances. In our patient, nausea and mild hyperactivity resolved spontaneously within a week. Nonetheless, caution is warranted in the acute phase of anti-NMDAR encephalitis, where receptor hypofunction predominates. Premature use of NMDAR antagonists may exacerbate symptoms in select cases [], therefore initiation after 12 to 18 months of conventional treatment, in post-acute phase, may have benefit on better and earlier improvement of cognitive sequalae, social and academic functioning.

[b00022] DISCUSSION
Although international guidelines stress early immunomodulation, they lack detailed recommendations for long-term cognitive rehabilitation []. This case supports incorporating pharmacological agents like memantine into individualized post-acute care plans, particularly for patients with plateaued recovery despite adequate immunotherapy. Notably, memantine may be associated with improved verbal fluency, sustained attention, social and academic functioning after 18 months of stabilization with conventional approaches, reinforcing the hypothesis that NMDAR modulation may aid cognitive restoration.

[b00023] DISCUSSION
This case further emphasizes that cognitive sequelae often persist even when psychiatric symptoms remit. Given the underlying mechanism of NMDAR internalization and disruption of synaptic plasticity, interventions like memantine that promote receptor regulation may be uniquely positioned to support recovery. Although current evidence in pediatric populations is limited, memantine appears tolerable and potentially beneficial. Its role in autoimmune encephalitis, while yet to be formally established, is biologically plausible and warrants further exploration. This case adds to emerging interest in glutamatergic agents for post-inflammatory neurocognitive recovery. Taken together, the addition of memantine as an adjuvant to conventional therapies in the post-acute phase of pediatric anti-NMDAR encephalitis may offer a potential approach for managing prolonged cognitive sequelae, particularly in cases with delayed initiation of standard treatments and more severe clinical presentations. However, the findings from this single case report—describing patient-specific characteristics—do not provide sufficient evidence to demonstrate the direct causality between cognitive improvement and memantine use, to generalize its efficacy, or to support a definitive recommendation for the use of memantine in similar cases. Longitudinal studies with larger pediatric samples and crossover designs are needed to evaluate the tolerability and effectiveness of memantine as an adjuvant in the treatment of pediatric anti-NMDAR encephalitis.

[b00024] DISCUSSION
In conclusion, memantine may serve as a promising adjunctive therapy for children with residual cognitive deficits after anti-NMDAR encephalitis. While this case provides early clinical insight into potential adjunctive strategies for addressing persistent cognitive sequelae in pediatric anti-NMDAR encephalitis, further observational studies with larger samples are needed before any formal treatment algorithm development can be considered. Prospective studies and controlled trials are needed to define its place within comprehensive neurorehabilitation protocols and improve long-term outcomes in this vulnerable population. Ultimately, such efforts could inform the development of targeted treatment algorithms and contribute to the optimization of long-term outcomes for children with autoimmune encephalitis.
