The utility of locking plate fixation combined with intraosseous wiring for an intra-articular depressed fracture of the middle phalangeal base: A case report

[b00001] Abstract
Comminuted depressed fractures at the base of the middle phalanx are often difficult to treat with a single fixation method, as achieving both anatomic reduction and stable fixation can be challenging. While soft stainless steel wire fixation and locking plate fixation have each been used individually as definitive treatments, no reports to date have described a combined approach. We present the case of a 74-year-old man with comminuted depressed fractures at the base of the middle and ring finger middle phalanges, successfully treated using a locking plate in combination with soft stainless steel wire. The addition of the wire allowed for temporary stabilization of fracture fragments, prevented displacement during screw insertion, and improved overall construct stability. This technique also enabled early postoperative rehabilitation to reduce the risk of adhesion. The combined use of soft stainless steel wire and locking plate fixation may represent a useful strategy for managing complex fractures of the middle phalanx base.

• Wire–plate combination achieved stable fixation in a complex phalangeal fracture. • Soft wire enabled temporary fixation of bone fragments during reduction. • Prevented fragment displacement and improved stability during screw insertion • Allowed early, proactive rehab to prevent postoperative adhesions and contractures • A novel strategy for treating comminuted depressed middle phalanx fractures

[b00003] Introduction
Comminuted depressed fractures of the base of the middle phalanx are among the most difficult phalangeal fractures to reduce and stabilize, and consistently achieving favorable clinical outcomes remains challenging. Delayed rehabilitation due to inadequate fixation can lead to tendon adhesions and joint contracture, resulting in persistent limitations in range of motion (ROM). To facilitate early rehabilitation, it is essential to restore the articular surface anatomically, re-establish concentric alignment of the proximal interphalangeal (PIP) joint, and achieve sufficient stability to withstand axial loading. Various treatment methods have been reported, including Kirschner wires [], screws [,], plates, stainless steel wires [], and external fixation [,]. However, no single method has been established as the gold standard, and this type of fracture remains difficult to manage.

[b00004] Introduction
Locking plates, which provide angular stability, have been reported to be effective for intra-articular fractures of the phalanges, with favorable clinical outcomes [,]. However, maintaining accurate reduction with temporary fixation without interfering with screw placement, or capturing small bone fragments with screws alone, can be technically demanding. The usefulness of soft stainless steel wires for the reduction and fixation of small bone fragments has been previously recognized []. More recently, the combination of temporary fixation using this technique with plate fixation has been reported as an effective approach []. However, to our knowledge, no reports have described the application of this technique to intra-articular fractures of the phalanges.

[b00005] Introduction
Herein, we report a case of a comminuted depressed fracture at the base of the middle phalanx involving both the middle and ring fingers in an elderly patient, which was successfully treated using locking plate fixation combined with soft stainless steel wires.

[b00006] Case presentation
A 74-year-old right-handed, unemployed man presented with comminuted fractures of the middle phalangeal bases of the right middle and ring fingers following a fall. Initial plain radiographs revealed comminuted depressed fractures at the base of the middle phalanges of both digits (Fig. 1). Computed tomography (CT) further demonstrated articular surface comminution and depression, as well as a dorsal fracture at the insertion of the central slip and a longitudinal fracture line extending into the diaphysis (Fig. 2). His medical history was significant only for hypertension, and he was a current smoker.

[b00007] Case presentation
Given the severity of the articular surface depression and the involvement of multiple fingers in an elderly patient, we judged that achieving accurate anatomic reduction and initiating early ROM exercises would be essential to preserve joint function. However, the use of traction-type external fixation was considered impractical due to the multi-digit involvement. Therefore, we planned open reduction and internal fixation using a locking plate to provide rigid fixation and enable early, simplified rehabilitation.

[b00008] Case presentation / Surgical technique
Surgery was performed under brachial plexus block anesthesia on day 3 after injury. The procedure is illustrated in Fig. 3. A dorsal midline longitudinal incision was made over the PIP joint. The dorsal base fragment of the middle phalanx was carefully flipped proximally while preserving the attachment of the central slip, thereby allowing direct visualization of the articular surface. With longitudinal traction applied to the affected digit, the depressed articular fragments were visualized and anatomically reduced by pressing them against the head of the proximal phalanx. To facilitate grasping of the comminuted bone fragments, two 0.7-mm drill holes were made in the diaphysis (only one for the ring finger), and 0.32-mm soft stainless steel wires were introduced through these holes using a 23-gauge needle as a guide. To support the subchondral bone following reduction, granular-form artificial bone (Superpore®, HOYA Corporation, Tokyo, Japan) was implanted beneath the articular fragments. A 0.32-mm wire was also passed through the central slip attachment on the dorsal fragment of the middle phalangeal base in preparation for later fixation.

[b00009] Case presentation / Surgical technique
Following reduction of the dorsal bone fragment, temporary stabilization was achieved by tightening the diaphyseal soft wires. While maintaining upward tension on the wire through the central slip, a 1.2-mm Y-shaped Aptus® Hand plate (Medartis AG, Basel, Switzerland) was placed dorsally. The plate was not strictly centered on the midline but rather positioned where it best secured the comminuted fragments. It was contoured as needed to match the bony surface. To avoid interference with lateral band gliding, the distal end of the plate was trimmed so that only two screws would be inserted into the distal fragment. After provisional plate fixation, cortical screws were used to compress the plate against the middle phalanx diaphysis. Locking screws were then inserted under fluoroscopic guidance to support the subchondral bone at optimal angles.

[b00010] Case presentation / Surgical technique
Finally, the soft wire passed through the central slip was tied to the plate, securing the dorsal base fragment of the middle phalanx. All wire ends were cut short and buried toward the bone to prevent interference with the lateral bands. Intraoperative fluoroscopy confirmed a smooth passive ROM of the PIP joint from 0° to 100°, with satisfactory stability of the fracture fragments. The periosteum was repaired as much as possible, and the skin was gently closed. Although the ring finger fracture was incomplete at the dorsal base of the middle phalanx, the same surgical technique was applied as described for the middle finger.

[b00011] Case presentation / Surgical technique
The total operative time was 3.5 h, with minimal intraoperative blood loss. Postoperative plain radiographs confirmed adequate reduction and fixation (Fig. 4).

[b00012] Case presentation / Postoperative course and follow-up
Gentle active ROM exercises were initiated on the first postoperative day. To prevent PIP joint extension lag, rehabilitation was performed using a relative motion extension protocol with the metacarpophalangeal (MP) joint held in flexion, similar to the rehabilitation strategy used for boutonnière deformity prevention, as described by Lalonde []. For the first 4 weeks postoperatively, the injured fingers were immobilized in extension during rest. Outside of exercise sessions, the PIP and distal interphalangeal (DIP) joints were maintained in full extension.

[b00013] Case presentation / Postoperative course and follow-up
CT at 3 months postoperatively confirmed good bone union, with fracture fragments and articular surfaces well stabilized by the combination of soft wire and locking plate fixation (Fig. 5). Although a slight residual step-off remained in the ring finger, both the middle and ring fingers maintained good concentric alignment.

[b00014] Case presentation / Postoperative course and follow-up
At the 12-month postoperative final follow-up, the patient demonstrated favorable ROM in each affected joint. In the middle finger, extension and flexion were 0°/96° at the MP joint, 10°/70° at the PIP joint, and − 30°/60° at the DIP joint. In the ring finger, the corresponding ranges were 4°/86° at the MP joint, 0°/86° at the PIP joint, and − 18°/60° at the DIP joint. Grip strength on the affected side measured 24 kg, which was 89 % of that on the contralateral side. A mild swan-neck deformity was noted in the middle finger, though the patient reported no pain (Fig. 6). Radiographic examination revealed mild osteoarthritic changes in the DIP joint, but no progression of degenerative changes in the PIP joint, where the joint space remained well preserved (Fig. 7). The patient experienced no discomfort from the implants and was able to carry out all activities of daily living without limitation.

[b00015] Discussion
Comminuted depressed fractures at the base of the middle phalanx often present a significant challenge in achieving both anatomical reduction and rigid fixation using a single fixation technique. While soft wire fixation and locking plate fixation have each been independently utilized as definitive treatments for such fractures, to the best of our knowledge, no prior reports have described the combination of these two methods. In this report, we presented a case of comminuted depressed fractures involving the middle phalangeal bases of multiple digits in an elderly patient. By combining locking plate fixation with soft wire fixation, we were able to achieve satisfactory anatomical reduction, rigid stabilization of small fracture fragments, and early initiation of ROM exercises. This strategy led to favorable radiographic and functional outcomes without complications such as implant-related discomfort or significant joint degeneration.

[b00016] Discussion
Various treatment methods have been reported for comminuted intra-articular fractures of the PIP joint, where early ROM is essential to prevent stiffness and preserve function under axial loading conditions [, , , , , ]. The advantages of K-wire or soft wire fixation include their low profile—resulting in minimal interference with surrounding tendinous structures—and their ability to secure small fracture fragments due to the thinness of the materials used. However, the fixation strength provided by these techniques alone is limited, making it difficult to achieve adequate stabilization of the articular surface, especially in cases with significant comminution [,]. Locking plate fixation, on the other hand, offers angular stability and more robust fixation of the articular surface. Nevertheless, it poses technical challenges in securing small, fragile fragments. Inserting screws into such fragments may result in displacement or fragmentation, particularly when the bone quality is poor or the fragments are thin [,]. The adjunctive use of soft wire with locking plate fixation provides a hybrid solution that capitalizes on the strengths of both techniques. Temporary stabilization using soft wire allows for secure reduction and maintenance of small fragments during screw insertion, reducing the risk of displacement. Additionally, this method enables enhanced overall construct stability, thereby supporting early mobilization protocols aimed at minimizing adhesion and joint contracture. This combined approach may be particularly useful in complex intra-articular fractures involving multiple digits or osteoporotic bone, as in our case.

[b00017] Discussion
In digital osteosynthesis, 28-gauge (0.32 mm) soft stainless steel wire is typically used for intraosseous wiring fixation []. Bone tunnels are first created using 0.9 mm or 0.7 mm Kirschner wires, after which the soft wire is passed through the prepared holes. When multiple wires are required, it is advisable to pass all the wires before performing any tying or fixation. Adding wires to volar fragments after reduction often necessitates extensive soft tissue dissection, thereby increasing surgical invasiveness and the risk of soft tissue complications. There are two primary techniques for passing soft wire through bone tunnels: directly threading the wire through the hole or using a 23-gauge needle as a guide for the soft wire (Table 1). In cases where the bone fragment is too small to be securely fixed with a wire, an alternative method involves anchoring the wire to attached soft tissues or ligaments, which can provide indirect yet effective stabilization []. When dual-directional fixation is required through a single bone tunnel, a soft wire can be folded in half, passed through the hole, and then cut to allow for securing in two separate directions []. The tension applied during wire tying can be finely adjusted, making soft wire a versatile tool in both provisional reduction and definitive fixation. This adaptability is particularly advantageous in managing complex phalangeal comminuted fractures, where fragment size, number, and shape can vary widely.

[b00020] Discussion
There are two main approaches to combining locking plate fixation with soft wire in fracture management. The first approach involves using soft wire for provisional fixation, followed by definitive plate fixation. In this method, soft wire is utilized to temporarily secure bone fragments, allowing for stabilization before applying the plate. In the present case, the comminuted fragments in the diaphyseal region were bundled together with intraosseous wiring to achieve provisional fixation. Because the soft wire is thin, it rarely interferes with plate placement or screw insertion. This feature facilitated the stabilization of fragments and improved the ease and reliability of the subsequent plate fixation. The second approach uses the plate itself as a support or anchor for soft wire fixation. After achieving axial stability with the plate, the soft wire is used to tie and connect comminuted fragments directly to the plate. In our case, the small dorsal basal fragment, which had been flipped proximally, was firmly fixed to the plate by tying the soft wire passed through the attachment site of the central slip. This technique enabled strong fixation of the comminuted area. By employing these two methods appropriately depending on the fracture situation, it is possible to achieve flexible yet stable fixation even for small intra-articular comminuted bone fragments.

[b00021] Discussion
In axial load-type fractures with a dorsal fracture line, flipping the dorsal fragment at its base, where the central slip inserts, allows for improved visualization and anatomical reduction. This approach is analogous to the olecranon osteotomy technique commonly used in intra-articular distal humerus fractures []. Although the Hintringer method involves blind reduction using Kirschner wire through a small cortical hole in the diaphysis without creating a cortical window, accurate anatomical reduction is often difficult to achieve [,]. Moreover, the present method allows for direct visualization and reduction of the articular fragments and facilitates grafting with artificial bone. For fixation of the flipped dorsal base fragment of the middle phalanx, the transtendinous wiring technique, which has proven effective in fixing small bone fragments in bony mallet injuries through the terminal tendon, is useful []. Passing a suture wire through the bone-tendon junction of the central slip and tensioning it distally to secure it to the plate provides sufficient stability. Postoperative rehabilitation should follow protocols similar to those used for buttonhole deformity, emphasizing relative flexion to facilitate smooth gliding of the lateral bands on the extensor side of the PIP joint [].

[b00022] Discussion
It has been pointed out that contact between dissimilar metals in the body can cause galvanic corrosion. However, previous studies have reported that the interaction between titanium and stainless steel in rabbits does not cause clinical problems [,]. In fracture treatment, it is common to use titanium plates in combination with stainless steel wires, and no clinical issues related to this combination have been reported []. In the present case, bone union was achieved without complications, and the benefits of combining these materials are considered significant in cases with severe comminution. Nevertheless, because the soft tissue on the dorsal aspect of the fingers is thin, if discomfort due to the plate or wire becomes problematic, implant removal should be considered.

[b00023] Discussion
Devices that allow combined fixation using soft wires and locking plates have been developed for use in the fixation of other long bones, such as the femur and clavicle []. However, no implants designed specifically for finger plates with built-in mechanisms for wire fixation currently exist. As further evidence accumulates regarding the utility of combining stainless steel wires with plates, it is expected that implants and treatment methods optimized for comminuted depressed fractures of the interphalangeal joints will be established.

[b00024] Conclusion
We reported a case of a depressed fracture at the base of the middle phalanx that was successfully treated with low-profile plate fixation combined with soft stainless steel wire. The use of wire in addition to the rigid fixation provided by the locking plate contributed to maintaining the reduction and enhancing stability, while also preventing fragment displacement during screw insertion. These advantages suggest that locking plate fixation combined with soft wire may serve as a valuable treatment option for depressed fractures of the middle phalangeal base.
