Atopic Dermatitis Successfully Treated with Lebrikizumab in Real-World Clinical Practice in Czech Republic: A Case Series

[b00001] Abstract
Atopic dermatitis (AD) is a chronic inflammatory skin disease associated with impaired quality of life and a substantial burden of disease. Lebrikizumab is a monoclonal antibody that selectively binds with high affinity to interleukin-13, thereby inhibiting its cascade signaling and reducing inflammation. Lebrikizumab has demonstrated efficacy and safety in adults and adolescents ≥ 12 years with moderate-to-severe AD in randomized, placebo-controlled, phase 3 clinical trials. Here, we report a case series of four patients with moderate-to-severe AD who transitioned to lebrikizumab treatment in the Czech Republic. All four patients had failed previous targeted therapies (biologics or Janus kinase inhibitors) and/or cyclosporine treatments and presented with associated comorbidities. After 12 to 16 weeks of treatment with lebrikizumab, clinically significant improvements in signs and symptoms (assessed by Eczema Area and Severity Index [EASI], pruritus Numerical Rate Scale, quality of life assessed by the Dermatology Life Quality Index [DLQI], and/or Patient Oriented Eczema Measure [POEM]) were reported. The results of these four clinical cases support the effectiveness observed in randomized clinical trials and suggest that lebrikizumab may be an effective treatment for moderate-severe AD in real-world clinical practice, even in patients with comorbidities who have failed previous advanced treatments.

[b00007] Introduction
Atopic dermatitis (AD) is a complex, immune-mediated, chronic inflammatory skin disease characterized by intense pruritus and recurrent dry, red, and excoriated lesions which can have a significant impact on quality of life (QoL) and emotional well-being [–].

[b00008] Introduction
The prevalence of AD varies in different countries and regions, with adult prevalence in Europe ranging from 1.2% to 8.7% [, ]. In the Czech Republic, the overall prevalence (2010 to 2021) ranged from 2.0% to 2.2% (4.7–5.8% in children and 1.4–1.5% in adults) [].

[b00009] Introduction
Emollients and topical corticosteroids are recommended first-line treatments for AD []. Until recently, the systemic treatment options available for severe AD included immunosuppressants, such as systemic corticosteroids, cyclosporine A, and off-label methotrexate or azathioprine [–]. Several advanced treatment options have been approved lately. In relation to this, lebrikizumab (LEB) is a novel, high-affinity monoclonal antibody that selectively binds to interleukin (IL)-13, the dominant skin cytokine in AD pathogenesis [–]. It exhibits high binding affinity, a slow disassociation rate, and neutralizes IL-13 with high potency []. Furthermore, it does not prevent the binding of IL-13 to the IL-13Rα2 receptor, allowing the internalization of IL-13 into the cell and thus not interfering with the endogenous regulation of IL-13 levels []. Lebrikizumab was authorized for use in the EU in November 2023 for the treatment of moderate-to-severe AD in adults and adolescents 12 years and older with a body weight of at least 40 kg who are candidates for systemic therapy. The recommended dose of LEB is 500 mg at both baseline and week 2, followed by 250 mg administered subcutaneously every other week up to week 16 [].

[b00010] Introduction
Although several randomized, placebo-controlled, phase 3 trials (RCTs) have demonstrated the efficacy and favorable safety profile of LEB in patients with moderate-to-severe AD [–], there is little data on the results of LEB treatment in moderate-to-severe AD in real-world clinical practice.

[b00011] Introduction
Here, we describe a series of four representative cases that reported moderate-to-severe AD, who had failed previous biologic and/or Janus kinase inhibitors (JAKi) and/or cyclosporine treatment options and were successfully treated with LEB in clinical practice in the Czech Republic.

[b00012] Introduction
Written informed consent was obtained from the patients for publication of this case series and any accompanying images. The study was conducted in strict accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, ensuring adherence to ethical standards and respecting patient privacy.

[b00013] Case Study 1
A 24-year-old woman presented with AD from 4 weeks of age and multiple atopic comorbidities (severe bronchial asthma, polyvalent allergies, and allergic rhinoconjunctivitis). In January 2024, the patient presented with erythema on all extremities, generalized skin xerosis and oozing, and she received the following treatments: cyclosporine 150 mg/day + amoxicillin/clavulanic acid, methylprednisolone 125 mg, levocetirizine, topical therapy, and zolpidem. In September 2024 (4 weeks after cyclosporine discontinuation), the patient started treatment with LEB + levocetirizine + topical therapy. Before initiation of LEB, the following scores were reported: Eczema Area and Severity Index (EASI) 23.0, Dermatology Life Quality Index (DLQI) 19.0, peak pruritus Numerical Rate Scale (NRS) score of 9.0, and Patient Oriented Eczema Measure (POEM) 21.0; her body surface area (BSA) of involvement was 80.0% (Fig. 1a). Improvements were observed as early as after 4 weeks of LEB treatment, with an EASI score of 10.0, DLQI 4.0, POEM 5.0, and peak pruritus NRS 3.0. At week 16, treatment with LEB showed a clinically significant decrease in AD severity, amelioration of pruritus, sleep, dyspnea, and rhinitis (EASI score of 3.0, DLQI 2.0, POEM 2.0, and peak pruritus NRS 1.0 (Fig. 1b); improvement versus baseline of 99.5%, 94.7%, 92.9%, and 100%, respectively). The involvement of BSA was reduced to 5.0%. The patient additionally reported relief in her dyspnea, rhinitis, and an amelioration of her sleep. The patient did not report any adverse event.

[b00014] Case Study 2
A 60-year-old woman presented with AD since she was 19 years old in combination with dermorespiratory syndrome. Prior treatments included dupilumab 300 mg Q2W (discontinued because of loss of efficacy), baricitinib 4 mg/day, upadacitinib 30 mg/day (discontinued because of pulmonary embolism), and azathioprine 100 mg/day (discontinued because of ineffectiveness). In September 2024, the patient started treatment with LEB. Before the treatment, the following scores were reported: EASI 24.3, DLQI 18.0, sleep NRS 8.0, and peak pruritus NRS 8.0; BSA of involvement was 22.0% (Fig. 2a). After 4 weeks of treatment, signs and symptoms and QoL were enhanced; the EASI score was reduced to 7.0, DLQI to 0.0, sleep NRS to 2.0, and peak pruritus NRS to 3.0. At week 16, the patient showed 100% improvement in all these scores (EASI, DLQI, and peak pruritus NRS scores 0.0 in all cases), and the involvement of BSA decreased to 0.0% (Fig. 2b). The patient did not report any adverse event.

[b00015] Case Study 3
A 24-year-old man presented with AD since childhood, with a mild form of bronchial asthma. He was hospitalized repeatedly. Prior treatments included external combined treatments (emollients, corticosteroids, and antibiotics) and cyclosporine (increasing dose from 150 mg/day in 2022 to 300 mg/day in 2024). In September 2024, after the discontinuation of cyclosporine, he started treatment with LEB. Before the treatment, the following scores were observed: EASI 36.8, DLQI 17.0, and peak pruritus NRS 8.0 (Fig. 3a), with an involvement of 90.0% BSA. At week 8, improvements were noticed in all scores assessed (EASI 16.4, DLQI 10.0, and pruritus NRS 5.0). After 16 weeks of treatment, significant clinical reductions were reported (EASI score of 4.4, DLQI 2.0, and peak pruritus NRS 1.0, Fig. 3b; improvement versus baseline of 88.0%, 88.0%, and 87.0%, respectively). The involvement of BSA decreased to 10.0%. The patient did not report any adverse event.

[b00016] Case Study 4
A 45-year-old woman was treated for severe AD since 2019 and hypofunctional autoimmune thyroiditis since she was 18 years old. She had received prior AD treatments including baricitinib 4 mg/day for 4 years (but then efficacy was lost), cyclosporine 200 mg/day, and upadacitinib 15 mg daily and later with 30 mg/day but with no efficacy. In September 2024, she started treatment with LEB. Before the treatment, the following scores were reported: EASI 28.1, DLQI 24.0, and peak pruritus NRS 10.0 (Fig. 4a); involvement of 70% BSA. Improvements were noticed as early as week 4 (EASI score reduction of 13.2, DLQI 18.0, and pruritus NRS 8.0). After 16 weeks of treatment, the patient showed clinically significant enhancements (EASI score of 2.4, DLQI 3.0, peak pruritus NRS 2.0, Fig. 4b; improvement versus baseline of 89.0%, 87.0% and 80.0%, respectively), including improvement of facial erythema. The involvement of BSA was reduced to 15.0%. The patient did not report any adverse event.

[b00017] Discussion
Biologic treatments target specific immune pathways in AD, reducing inflammation and improving skin barrier function, with a high benefit-to-risk ratio, high efficacy, and acceptable safety profiles. Among them, LEB is a newly available treatment for moderate-to-severe AD. A systematic review and meta-analysis that included seven RCTs assessing the efficacy and safety of IL-13 inhibitors in AD revealed that IL-13 antagonists such as LEB demonstrated encouraging clinical efficacy against moderate-to-severe AD [].

[b00018] Discussion
The use of stringent inclusion and exclusion criteria in RCTs often leads to the underrepresentation of certain patient populations typically encountered in routine clinical practice, such as patients with multiple comorbidities, extensive prior treatment histories, or those receiving polypharmacy. Consequently, real-world studies are essential for ongoing evaluation of drug effectiveness and safety in heterogeneous patient populations and varied clinical environments. Real-world evidence further supports informed therapeutic decision-making; however, there is little information published about the use of LEB in moderate-to-severe AD in real-world clinical practice.

[b00019] Discussion
This four-case series had moderate-to-severe AD, failed previous AD treatments (including biologics, JAKi, or cyclosporine) and reported associated comorbidities such as asthma and allergies. Our results are directly supported by the first Czech real-world analysis from the BIOREP registry, which evaluated 181 patients with moderate-to-severe AD treated with LEB between September and December 2024 []. While the BIOREP study demonstrated higher response rates in treatment-naïve patients (EASI-90, 41.9% vs 22.9% in dupilumab failures; p < 0.05), our case series shows that LEB is also effective in patients with previous advance treatments and multiple comorbidities. Specifically, our patients achieved EASI improvements ranging from 87.0% to 99.5%, DLQI reductions of 87.0–94.7%, and pruritus NRS improvements of 80.0–100% at 16 weeks and represent substantial clinical benefits despite prior treatment failures.

[b00020] Discussion
The presence of comorbidities may lead to worsening of the underlying AD in some patients; however, the four patients showed clinically meaningful improvement in all measurements: EASI, DLQI, pruritus NRS, and/or POEM scores and a reduction of the percentage of BSA involved following treatment with LEB for 12 or 16 weeks (even in most cases at week 4), supporting evidence from the ADvocate trials in which 72.0% of patients had atopic comorbidities with sustained effectiveness regardless of comorbidity status []. During the first weeks of lebrikizumab treatment, all four patients continued with only topical therapy, which had previously shown no effect. Therefore, it is unlikely that the rapid improvement, reported as early as week 4, was due to the topical therapy alone. Notably, patient 1 reported concurrent improvements in dyspnea and rhinitis alongside AD control. These preliminary observations may suggest a potential role for selective IL-13 blockade in improving mild to moderate allergic comorbidities, although further data in a wider population and with a proper evaluation with available questionnaires will be needed to confirm it.

[b00021] Discussion
Several limitations should be acknowledged. The small sample size limits generalizability, though the consistent responses across all four cases and alignment with larger registry data support the clinical validity of our observations. This observational design prevents direct comparison with control groups, but the substantial improvements from baseline in validated outcome measures provide robust evidence of treatment benefit. In this regard, the photographed regions of patient 2 do not completely correspond to before and after treatment; however, we believe that they are representative of the clinical scores observed across all four patients. Additionally, the 12–16-week follow-up period, while sufficient to demonstrate short-term efficacy, requires longer observation to confirm sustained effectiveness and long-term safety in real-world settings.

[b00022] Conclusions
These four cases demonstrate that LEB can provide clinically meaningful benefits in patients with moderate-to-severe AD who have failed multiple advanced therapies, including biologics and JAK inhibitors. The outcomes align with recent Czech BIOREP registry data while highlighting the therapeutic potential of LEB in treatment-refractory populations. Combined with evidence of concurrent improvement in atopic comorbidities and favorable safety profiles, these findings support LEB as a valuable addition to the therapeutic armamentarium for patients with complex AD. Future larger-scale studies and longer-term follow-up are needed to further characterize optimal patient selection and treatment sequencing strategies.
