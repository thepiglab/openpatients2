Analysis of three cases of disseminated BCG infection in infants after BCG vaccination in Beijing, China

[b00001] ABSTRACT
We report and analyze three cases of disseminated BCG infection in infants after BCG vaccination that occurred between 2022 and 2024 in Chaoyang District, Beijing, which were the only three monitored during the clinical application we conducted. All the patients had primary immunodeficiency disease (PID). Two cases had defects in the JAK3 and TNFRSF13B genes; one case had multiple gene defects in CR2, IFNAR2, TLR2, and exon 13 of DOCK11. Disseminated BCG infection is a rare adverse reaction after BCG vaccination. It occurs almost exclusively in immunodeficient infants or children. Although it rarely occurs, it is necessary to pay more attention to clinical practice because of its serious consequences.

[b00002] Introduction
The Bacillus Calmette–Guérin (BCG) vaccine was first used in humans in 1921.^(,) It is a live attenuated vaccine derived from Mycobacterium bovis strain. BCG vaccine is recommended to prevent tuberculosis (TB), especially TB meningitis and miliary TB in newborns or children, without a booster dose. The meta-analysis conducted by Trunz BB et al. indicated that the BCG vaccine’s protective effect is 73% (95% CI: 67–79) against TB meningitis and 77% (95% CI: 58–87) against miliary TB.^(,) In 1974, the World Health Organization (WHO) incorporated the vaccine in the global Expanded Program of Immunization (EPI). To date, more than 150 countries worldwide have implemented universal BCG vaccination programs. BCG vaccination was initiated in 1956 in China, and was incorporated into the National Immunization Program (NIP) in 1978.

[b00003] Introduction
As with other vaccines, adverse reactions do occur inevitably after BCG vaccination. The common adverse reactions monitored in our clinical application mainly included injection site reactions (redness, swelling, abscesses, and ulceration at the injection site), regional lymphadenitis, transient fever after inoculation, and allergic rash, which are consistent with other reports.^(,,,) Rare osteomyelitis is also stated in the instructions, but no case has been monitored during the clinical application.

[b00004] Introduction
Disseminated BCG infection is also a rare adverse reaction.^(,) It can manifest with systemic symptoms including fever, weight loss, irritability and more than two sites of BCG complications distal to the BCG vaccine injection site involving the skin, lymph node, lung, bone, or liver with a documented isolation of Mycobacterium bovis and a histological finding on the tissue biopsy.^(,,) Disseminated BCG infection occurs between 1.56 and 4.29 cases per million doses, and its mortality rate is as high as 60–80%.^(,)

[b00005] Introduction
The purpose of this study was to report and analyze three cases of disseminated BCG infection in infants after BCG vaccination that occurred between 2022 and 2024, which are the only three cases monitored after BCG vaccination during the clinical application we conducted. Although it rarely occurs, it is necessary to pay more attention to clinical practice because of the serious consequences of disseminated BCG infection and to provide suggestions for the adjustment of BCG vaccination strategies in China.

[b00006] Materials and methods / Presentation of three cases
Case 1: A male infant was administered one dose of BCG vaccine on November 29, 2022 (within 24 hours of birth). The BCG vaccine was manufactured by Shanghai Institute of Biology Co., Ltd., and the dose was 0.1 mL containing 0.05 mg of BCG. The BCG vaccine was administered intracutaneously in the deltoid muscle of the left upper arm.

[b00007] Materials and methods / Presentation of three cases
On March 29, 2023, the infant had sudden, unprovoked bouts of high fever (38°C). On April 4, 2023, humoral immunity was examined and found to be decreased, and there was a zero count for both T cells and NK cells. Genetic analysis on April 11, 2023 revealed that the infant had JAK3-associated severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID). There were two heterozygous mutations in the JAK3 gene: (1) c.308 G > A (exon 3, NM_000215.4) led to a change in the amino acid p.Arg103His, which was a missense mutation. According to the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG) guidelines, the variant was determined to be a suspected pathogenic variant PM1+PP3+PM3_Strong+PM2_Supporting. (2) c.307C > T (exon 3, NM_000215.4) led to a change in the amino acid p.Arg103Cys, which was a missense mutation. According to the ACMG guidelines, the variant was determined to be a suspected pathogenic variant PM1+PP3+PM3_Strong+PM2_Supporting. Mycobacterium tuberculosis complex group infection was observed via next-generation sequencing (NGS) on April 12, 2023. The infant was treated with anti-tuberculosis drugs, and he received hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) on July 11, 2023. The infant was treated with rifampicin (16 mg/kg/day, orally twice a day), isoniazid (11 mg/kg/day, orally once a day), and linezolid (10 mg/kg/day, venous transfusion). Five days later, the linezolid was adjusted to oral administration (30 mg/kg/day, orally three times a day). In addition, isoniazid and linezolid were used for long-term treatment. The infant recovered well.

[b00008] Materials and methods / Presentation of three cases
In addition, the infant had adverse reactions at the injection site. In April 2023, the abscess at the injection site burst, and pus flowed. Cleaning of sores and suture surgery were performed on June 21, 2023, and white cheesy material was visible during the surgery.

[b00009] Materials and methods / Presentation of three cases
Case 2: A female infant was administered one dose of BCG vaccine on September 3, 2022 (within 24 hours of birth). The BCG vaccine was manufactured by Shanghai Institute of Biology Co., Ltd., and the dose was 0.1 mL containing 0.05 mg of BCG. The BCG vaccine was administered intracutaneously in the deltoid muscle of the left upper arm.

[b00010] Materials and methods / Presentation of three cases
On October 28, 2022, she was administered one dose of a reassortant rotavirus vaccine, live, oral, pentavalent (Vero cells). The reassortant rotavirus vaccine was manufactured by Merk Sharp & Dohme Corp., and the dose was 2 mL containing 2.2 × 10^(6) U reassortant rotavirus G1, 2.8 × 10^(6) U reassortant rotavirus G2, 2.2 × 10^(6) U reassortant rotavirus G3, 2.0 × 10^(6) U reassortant rotavirus G4, and 2.3 × 10^(6) U reassortant rotavirus P1A[8].

[b00011] Materials and methods / Presentation of three cases
The day after the reassortant rotavirus vaccination (October 29, 2022), rashes were found on the infant’s back and hands. The rashes then spread throughout the body, accompanied by fever, cough, and lymphadenopathy (left armpit and bilateral groin). On November 29, 2022, blood tests indicated decreased levels of IgA, IgG, and IgM, and the proportions of inhibitory T-cell and B lymphocyte subsets were significantly reduced. Genetic analysis on November 2, 2022 revealed that the infant had TNFRSF13B-associated SCID. There was one heterozygous mutation in the TNFRSF13B gene, according to the ACMG guidelines, and c.105del (p.Glu36LysfsTer48) was determined to be a suspected pathogenic variant PVS1+PM2_Supporting. Mycobacterium tuberculosis complex infection was detected in the blood, marrow, and alveolar lavage fluid via NGS on January 10, 2023, and the type was bovine.

[b00012] Materials and methods / Presentation of three cases
The infant was treated with rifampicin (16 mg/kg/day, orally once a day), isoniazid (15 mg/kg/day, orally once a day), ethambutol (15 mg/kg/day, orally once a day), linezolid (10 mg/kg/day, orally three times a day), and levofloxacin (8 mg/kg/day, venous transfusion). In addition, she received HSCT on May 25, 2023. She died on October 2, 2023. On the basis of SCID, mixed infections of multiple pathogens (Pneumocystis jirovecii, Mycobacterium tuberculosis complex, Mycoplasma pneumoniae, Rotavirus group A, Enterobacter Hornii, Enterobacter cloaca, novel coronavirus Omicron BF.7, Epstein – Barr virus, fungi, norovirus) occurred repeatedly and then septic shock, pulmonary hemorrhage, acute respiratory distress syndrome, and systemic multi-organ and multi-system failure, which led to death.

[b00013] Materials and methods / Presentation of three cases
Case 3: A male infant was administered one dose of BCG vaccine on June 2, 2024 (within 24 hours of birth). The BCG vaccine was manufactured by Chengdu Institute of Biological Products Co., LTD., and the dose was 0.1 mL containing 0.05 mg of BCG. The BCG vaccine was administered intracutaneously in the deltoid muscle of the left upper arm.

[b00014] Materials and methods / Presentation of three cases
On June 8, 2024, the infant had sudden hypersomnia and ashen complexion. In addition, he had a fever (37.4℃) on July 1, 2024. On July 4, 2024, immune function of lymphocyte test exhibited decreased CD4/CD8 double-positive cells, B lymphocytes (CD19+ and CD3−), initial B lymphocytes (CD27− and IgD+), memory B lymphocytes (CD27+ and IgD−), plasmablasts (CD38+ and CD24−), and transitional B lymphocytes (CD24+ and CD38+); total T lymphocytes (CD3+ and CD19−), helper T lymphocytes (CD3+ and CD4+), and primary helper T lymphocytes (CD27+ and CD45RA−) were all increased. Genetic testing on July 16, 2024 indicated that there were missense mutations in CR2 (NM_0010066583: c.1724 G > A: p.R575H), IFNAR2 (NM_207585.3: c.1240A > T: p.T414S), and TLR2 (NM_003264.5: c.1039A > G: p.K347E) genes, and exon 13 of the DOCK11 gene was suspected to be duplicated (chrx: 117712488–117712610-dup), indicating the existence of immunodeficiency. Mycobacterium tuberculosis complex group infection was observed via NGS on July 8, 2024. The infant was treated with rifampicin (12 mg/kg/day, taken all at once in the morning), isoniazid (12 mg/kg/day, taken all at once in the morning), pyrazinamide (25 mg/kg/day, orally twice a day), and linezolid (10 mg/kg/day, venous transfusion). One week later, linezolid was administered orally (10 mg/kg/day, orally three times a day). Two weeks later, the infant recovered well. In addition, rifampicin, isoniazid and linezolid were used for long-term treatment.

[b00023] Materials and methods / Investigation and diagnosis of adverse events following immunization (AEFI)
According to the requests of the guardians, to determine the causal association between the disseminated BCG infection in infants and BCG vaccination, doctors in Chaoyang CDC thoroughly investigated the cases roundly.

[b00024] Materials and methods / Investigation and diagnosis of adverse events following immunization (AEFI)
Experts in vaccinology, epidemiology, pediatrics, phthisiology, and infectiology investigated and diagnosed the cases. In addition, the experts thoroughly reviewed the clinical features of the cases, the results of the medical examination/test, and the characteristics of the BCG vaccine. Finally, the experts reached a consensus after comprehensive evaluation, analysis, and full discussion.

[b00025] Materials and methods / Principle of causal association of adverse events following immunization
The causal association of the cases was evaluated mainly according to the “Causal Assessment of Adverse Events after Immunization” (2019) of the WHO and “Vaccine Administration Law of the People’s Republic of China.”

[b00026] Materials and methods / Principle of causal association of adverse events following immunization
The principle of causal association of adverse events following immunization includes the following: Is there strong evidence for other causes? Is there a known causal association with vaccines/vaccinations? Is there strong evidence that does not support causality? Review other factors affecting classification.

[b00027] Results
The clinical diagnosis of the three cases was disseminated BCG infection. In case 2, the causal association between disseminated BCG infection and BCG vaccination was confirmed. In the other two cases, the causal association was inconclusive but could not be disregarded.

[b00028] Discussion
Disseminated BCG infection is a rare but serious adverse reaction after BCG vaccination. It occurs almost exclusively in immunodeficient infants or children.^(,,) A retrospective cross-sectional study in Oman included 226 children younger than 13 years with complications of BCG vaccination from 2006 to 2018, and of 11 children with disseminated BCG infection, 72.7% (n = 8) had PID. In the prospective study conducted by E. Bukhari et al., 12 patients with disseminated BCG infection after BCG vaccination were diagnosed from 2015 to 2016 at three tertiary care hospitals in Riyadh (Saudi Arabia), and all of them had an underlying immune deficiency. Also, in a similar study from Iran, eight patients were diagnosed with disseminated BCG infection, and all patients had SCID. Related studies mostly come from Africa, Asia, and South America, as the BCG vaccine is not routinely administered in the United States, Canada and most European countries, which may lead to an underrepresentation of cases.

[b00029] Discussion
Existing publications on the causal association between disseminated BCG infection and BCG vaccination in PID infants are lacking. This paper states specifically that there is a causal association between disseminated BCG infection and BCG vaccination in infants suffering from PID, and it provides suggestions for the adjustment of BCG vaccination strategies in China.

[b00030] Discussion
PID refers to inborn errors of immunity (IEIs), by definition, caused by damaging germline variants in single genes. The clinical manifestations include susceptibility to infections, autoimmune diseases, allergy, and malignancy. PID includes SCID, X-linked agammaglobulinaemia (LXA), common variable immunodeficiency (CVID), selective IgA deficiency (SIgAD), Wiskott – Aldrich syndrome (WAS), and chronic granulomatous diseases (CGD). It has been reported that infants/children with disseminated BCG infection often have simultaneous presence of SCID, CGD, complete DiGeorge syndrome (CDGS), Mendelian susceptibility to mycobacterial disease (MSMD), or other diseases.^(,,) The advent of NGS technology enhanced the discoveries in the field of PID. At present, 508 genes that cause 559 disorders have been described. Consistent with others’ reports, the aforementioned three cases had JAK3, TNF and TLR2 defects, which increased their susceptibility to Mycobacterium tuberculosis.

[b00031] Discussion
Furthermore, the study of Sellami et al. indicated that BCG vaccine complications should be suspected in any skin lesions after BCG vaccination, because they may be accompanied by a systemic infection dissemination. However, of the three cases reported in this paper, only one had an abscess at the injection site. It indicates that even if there is no adverse reaction at the injection site after BCG vaccination, the possibility of disseminated BCG infection should be noted.

[b00032] Discussion
Anti-tuberculosis drugs and immune reconstitution by HSCT are key factors in the treatment of disseminated BCG infection in patients with PID. Isoniazid combined with rifampicin is a cornerstone of multidrug treatment. The WHO consolidated guidelines on tuberculosis focused on the 6-month treatment regimen composed of four first-line TB medications – isoniazid, rifampicin, ethambutol and pyrazinamide – recommended for the treatment of drug-susceptible TB. There is no standard treatment regimen for disseminated BCG infection with PID. Nevertheless, the individual’s sensitivity and drug resistance should be fully considered before drug treatment.

[b00033] Discussion
Therefore, parents need to observe infants closely for symptoms after BCG vaccination, such as injection site reactions (redness, swelling, abscesses, and ulceration at the injection site), regional lymphadenitis, fever, and inflammation. Timely medical treatment should be sought when discomfort occurs, as early identification and appropriate treatment can change the outcomes of serious adverse reactions, especially disseminated BCG infection. And that infants once diagnosed as disseminated BCG infection, it should also be alerted for an underlying PID.

[b00034] Discussion
Based on the emergence of the aforementioned cases in the last 3 years, the BCG vaccination in Chaoyang District, Beijing, is facing new challenges.

[b00035] Discussion
The Global Tuberculosis Report of the WHO stated that TB remains one of the global public health problems, and it is the ninth leading cause of death worldwide. The BCG vaccine plays an important role in preventing TB, especially TB meningitis and miliary TB in newborns or children. In addition, vaccination policies vary across countries, mainly depending on the prevalence of TB. TB-endemic areas (mainly in developing countries) have adopted universal vaccination, whereas in the low-prevalence countries, the BCG vaccine is recommended for individuals in high-risk groups.^(,) In China, the BCG vaccine has been administered nationwide to all newborns since 1978. Furthermore, in recent years, TB has been effectively controlled. Meanwhile, with the rising of serious adverse reactions, is universal BCG vaccination still necessary? China is a large country, and its natural and socio-economic conditions among various regions are strikingly different, so we suggest that regional and nearby areas’ TB prevalence and migration patterns between areas should be assessed strictly to properly develop BCG vaccination policies properly.

[b00036] Discussion
In addition, the appropriate age for BCG vaccination is also worth further exploration. Japan amended the recommended age for BCG vaccination to 5–8 months in 2013 because of the increasing incidence of BCG-associated osteitis/osteomyelitis. The surveillance study of Hideo Okuno et al. in Japan from 2013 to 2017 indicated that the incidence of BCG-associated osteitis/osteomyelitis and disseminated BCG infection in the population vaccinated at younger than 6 months was higher than that in the population vaccinated at older than 6 months, and the rates were 3.8 per million (younger than 6 months)/1.9 per million (older than 6 months) and 1.3 per million (younger than 6 months)/0.6 per million (older than 6 months) in the two aforementioned diseases. A study conducted in Saudi Arabia suggested that BCG vaccination should be postponed until the child is 1 year old, as most of the congenital immunodeficiency diseases are apparent by 1 year of age. Studies conducted in Oman and Kuwait have proposed to delay the BCG vaccination from birth to 6 months old.^(,) In China, BCG vaccination is usually performed within 24 hours after birth. Based on the characteristics of PID, we suggest that BCG vaccination should be postponed to avoid serious adverse reactions. In addition, the appropriate age for BCG vaccination requires further exploration.

[b00037] Discussion
At present, various BCG strains are used in the BCG vaccine. Among them, four strains (Danish strain 1331, Tokyo strain 172, Pasteur strain 1173 P2, and Glaxo strain 1077) are the most common types worldwide. However, no BCG strain has been found to be markedly superior to other strains and there is no global consensus on the choice of an optimal strain for general use. In China, the Shanghai D_(2)PB302 strain is used for the production of the BCG vaccine, which is a descendant strain of the Danish 823 strain. Whether there are safer and more effective strains for the production of BCG vaccine requires further research. On the other hand, can new vaccines be developed to improve their safety and effectiveness? Other researchers have reported that subunit vaccines (attenuated viral vector or adjuvanted fusion protein) and whole-cell vaccines (genetically attenuated Mycobacterium tuberculosis, recombinant BCG, killed Mycobacterium tuberculosis or Mycobacterium vaccine) have being studied.

[b00038] Discussion
Another point that needs to be considered is that newborn screening should be performed before BCG vaccination to find serious diseases, especially in newborns with high-risk factors (familial inheritance, underweight, overweight, maternal history of adverse pregnancy and delivery). Generally, infants with severe immunodeficiency are usually asymptomatic at birth, and they have high risk of developing fatal disseminated BCG infection. Advances in molecular biology and biotechnology have allowed the identification of infants/children with severe forms of PID manifested by T- and B-cell lymphopenia. Newborn screening programs for PID include T-cell receptor excision circles (TRECs) and kappa-deleting recombination excision circles (KRECs). Newborn screening was first introduced in the United States. In recent years, many countries worldwide, including a growing number of European countries, have implemented newborn screening. Newborn screening has many advantages. On the one hand, newborns with positive results will still be asymptomatic, so treatment is likely to be earlier and more successful. On the other hand, postponing the administration of live attenuated vaccines to prevent the occurrence of vaccine-related diseases will greatly protect infants/children, as some diseases are fatal, such as disseminated BCG infection and poliomyelitis.

[b00039] Conclusions
Disseminated BCG infection is a rare adverse reaction after BCG vaccination. It occurs almost exclusively in immunodeficient infants or children. Although it occurs rarely, it is necessary to pay more attention to clinical practice because of its serious consequences.
