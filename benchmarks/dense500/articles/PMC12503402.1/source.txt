Endoscope-assisted revision surgery for cochlear implant electrode array exposure: a case report

[b00002] None
Electrode exposure is a rare, potentially severe delayed complication following cochlear implant surgery.[] Prompt surgical explantation and reimplantation or revision surgery with the aid of a microscope to repair the defect or close the external auditory canal to retain the cochlear implant are effective remedies.[] We present the first case, to the best of our knowledge, wherein the patient was treated by endoscope-assisted revision surgery via the posterior external auditory canal to cover the exposed electrode array with a cartilage/tragal perichondrium graft.

[b00003] None
Our patient, a 46-year-old man, had a cochlear implant placed in the right ear in 2013 at our clinic because of profound binaural sensorineural hearing loss. Five years after the cochlear implant, the patient presented with otorrhoea of 8 months duration in the implanted ear. The implant had been functioning normally, but otoscopic examination revealed an electrode array protruding through the external auditory canal. The patient agreed to endoscopic revision surgery to cover the exposed electrode in the mastoid cavity and to repair the broken posterior wall of the external ear canal.

[b00004] None
A composite tragal cartilage–perichondrium graft was harvested and submerged in normal saline. Ear wax, pus and scabs were removed endoscopically, and the external auditory canal, mastoid cavity and exposed electrode [Figure 1] were disinfected by washing three times with compound lysostaphin disinfectant. The soft tissue around the electrode was cleaned to release the electrode array and expose the mastoid cavity [Figure 2a]. Exploration via the facial recess confirmed that the electrode array was well positioned within the cochlea [Figure 2b]. The exposed electrode array was carefully rotated to reverse its spiral movement and then gently pushed back into the mastoid cavity. The mucosa around the broken external ear canal wall was carefully scarified with a serrated circular knife to prepare a bed for the graft [Figure 2c]. To repair the external ear canal wall, the composite tragal cartilage–perichondrium graft was inserted through the perforation with the perichondrium facing the external auditory canal [Figure 2d]. The meatus was packed with gelfoam and an iodoform gauze strip. The entire procedure was performed under endoscopic guidance.

[b00005] None
The performance of the right implant was the same as it was before the revision surgery. Outpatient follow-up 12 months after surgery confirmed the integrity of the external auditory canal repair [Figure 3].

[b00006] None
Compared with other procedures, endoscope-assisted revision surgery shows some advantages, including successful salvage of functional implants, less surgical trauma and less risk of damaging the electrode because the surgeon can clearly see the lesion around the electrode. In our experience, it is important to adjust the electrode coil to eliminate excess pressure on the bony wall. The operator needs to thoroughly clean away the diseased tissue around the electrodes to provide a good transplant bed for the graft. The graft also affects the outcome. Most of the grafts used to repair the external auditory canal are composites of tragal cartilage and perichondrium because the tissue is tough enough to avoid relapse. Extreme caution must be exercised to avoid dislodging the implant during the procedure.

[b00007] None
Compared to transmastoid repair, there are also limitations in the surgery progress, such as limited visualisation to the underside of the lateral edge of the defect and the mastoid cavity lateral, which increases the difficulty in clearing the unhealthy tissue/squames in the mastoid. Depending on the condition, a deep consideration is needed to decide whether the endaural or postaural approach is appropriate. Although there is only one case that demonstrated this surgical process, this report suggests another method to manage the complication of cochlear implant electrode array exposure. As the initial electrode extrusion took years, the long-term outcomes of this surgical procedure should be followed.
