Extract explicit outcomes and clinical-course events, not imagined endpoints or recommendations.

Distinguish improvement, recovery, deterioration, recurrence, readmission, complications, discharge, follow-up, loss to follow-up, and death. Preserve the outcome's related condition, disposition, explicitly documented cause, time anchor, and follow-up duration. 'Discharged in stable condition' is not necessarily cured. 'No recurrence at six months' is an explicit absent recurrence at that follow-up, not lifetime absence of recurrence. A follow-up planned in six months is a care plan, not an observed outcome.

Death must be an event in the index patient's described clinical course. A pre-existing cadaver, a relative's death, a general mortality statement, a hypothetical prognosis, or a specimen report does not establish an index-patient clinical death outcome. Stillbirth, pregnancy termination, neonatal death, and adult death have different subjects and contexts; preserve these rather than merging them.

Do not calculate overall survival, progression-free survival, time to treatment, censoring dates, or causal treatment effects. Do not assume that the last mentioned encounter is the end of observation. Censoring/lost-to-follow-up is recorded only when explicitly described, not inferred from a narrative stopping. A treatment preceding improvement does not by itself prove causality; cause_as_documented requires explicit source attribution.

Keep progression/response assessments and different follow-up times separate. Negative or uncertain outcomes require qualifiers in evidence. Missing outcomes are not favorable outcomes. Broader outcome states should use event=other with precise text rather than force an inaccurate category.

Positive: "Died on hospital day 5" supports death with that relative time, not the publication date. Explicit negative: "No recurrence at 12-month follow-up" is an absent recurrence at that follow-up only, not a cure or lifelong absence. Missing: no follow-up produces items=[], not_documented, not survival, discharge, recovery, or absence of adverse events. Loss to follow-up is not death. "No change" is not necessarily improvement or absence of disease. Preserve follow-up duration and related condition only where explicitly stated; never calculate survival time from a deidentified or unrelated date.
